# 49GAION Standard

**Version `v1.7.0` · approved by the governing seat (GOV) of 49GAION.** Decisions are recorded in `decisions/README.md`.

The 49GAION Standard defines what 49GAION is, its product, its units, its parts and the rules that must hold. It is a definition, not an operating manual: how a particular organisation runs is set in that organisation's own `[ORG]AION`.

Companions: the master definition (`49GAION.md`), the 49GAION Naming Standard (`standards/49GAION-NAMING-STANDARD.md`), the 49GAION Operating Protocol (`49g-holding/49GAION-protocol`), the AIOC definition and blueprint template (`49g-holding/49GAION-aioc`). Where a companion and this Standard differ, this Standard governs.

> **No natural person (DR-0013).** In 49GAION and in every `[ORG]AION`, a natural person does not exist. Every human in the system is a **position**: a legal persona with its own code. The link between a position and the natural persons who occupy it belongs only to `[ORG]HOAC-TM`, in its own mechanism, and no AI part ever touches it. The rule is §1.1, item 9; every other text of 49GAION is read with it.

> **The human is present, as a legal persona (DR-0015).** It is not that the human does not exist in the system. Every human is present, and decides, signs and is accountable, as a position: a legal persona whose nature is human, known by its legal character. The natural person behind it exists, outside the system's knowledge; only `[ORG]HOAC-TM` knows it and links it, in its own mechanism. Two positions are two parties, even when one natural person occupies both, and every act stays with its position. The rule is §1.1, items 9 and 10.

## 1. What 49GAION is

### 1.1 Definition

1. **Category.** 49GAION is an **organisation design framework**: a framework for building organisations from interchangeable AI and human parts. AION stands for AI Operating Nexus.
2. **Philosophy.** 49GAION is designed AI-first: AI does the work by default, and humans take part where they are needed, as accountable overseers. AI-first is the framework's internal design philosophy, not part of its category or its name.
   - **Addition (DR-0031): AI-native.** 49GAION designs AI-native organisations. An AI-native organisation is designed from the start so that AI produces its output. Inside the work, humans increase the power of AI and cover its gaps; above the work, humans own the organisation, set its output, its quality and its limits, and answer for it. A human seat is accountable for every output, and no AI accepts accountability (HF-01). AI-first, in this item and in the glossary, reads as AI-native. Like AI-first, AI-native is part of neither the category nor the name. The full text is in the master definition, `49GAION.md`, sections 1 and 2.
3. **Output.** The output of 49GAION is an organisation design, delivered as its product, `[ORG]AION`.
   - **Reading (DR-0035).** The product is the organisation's living core, the branded AION, which holds the organisation's design and builds from it (§1.3, item 1).
4. **The product.** 49GAION's product is `[ORG]AION`, the AI Operating Nexus of one organisation. Its definition is held once, in §1.3, item 1.
5. **Nobody runs on 49GAION directly.** Every organisation runs on its own `[ORG]AION`. Outside text names the organisation's `[ORG]AION` or AIOC and adds only "Powered by 49GAION".
6. **One kind of unit, composed without limit.** The unit level holds the AIOCs and how they work together. An AIOC may hold other AIOCs, to any depth, with no limit (§1.7). Inside an AIOC, its Blueprint holds its Pods and Cells.
7. **Generic.** The Standard names no organisation, no person and no tool. Organisation names, holders of seats, tools and exact values belong to registers and to each `[ORG]AION`.
8. **What the framework comprises.** 49GAION comprises the **AIOCs** (its units), the **Protocol** (how they work together), the **Blueprint** (the one file that defines each AIOC with its Pods and Cells) and the six parts in §1.2 that hold and govern them.
9. **No natural person (DR-0013).** The direction of GOV of 15 October 2026: "In the AION system the human as a natural person does not exist at all. Every human that exists, exists as a legal persona. Where legal personas are linked to natural persons, that has its place only in top management, belongs to it, has its own mechanism, and has nothing to do with you." (translated)
   - **Only positions.** A human exists in the system only as a position: a legal persona with its own code, carrying a HOAC label (§4.1; §5.2, Position Principle). Every human act in the system (deciding, signing, approving, answering, contributing, being accountable) is the act of a position.
   - **Nowhere a natural person.** No Blueprint, card, Work Item, register entry read by a part, record, prompt, instruction or AI memory names, addresses, describes or reasons about a natural person who acts in the system.
   - **The link is top management's alone.** The link between a position and the natural persons who occupy it belongs only to `[ORG]HOAC-TM`, under `[ORG]HOAC-GOV`, in its own mechanism: the arrangement of each position, and the occupancy and handover entries of the private register copy (§5.2, Arranging power and Register entry labels). For `[ORG]HOAC-TM` itself, GOV holds that link; for GOV, the founding configuration.
   - **No AI part touches it.** No AIOC, Pod, Cell, bot or other AI part holds, reads, writes, stores or infers that link. An AI part addresses a position, through that position's HU port; whoever occupies the position under the arrangement in force receives it there. Only the tool gateway checks, at sign-in and at signing, that a live sign-in belongs to a current occupant, and it writes the occupant IDs of a decision stamp into top management's records alone.
   - **Content is not an actor.** The rule is about who acts in the system. An organisation's own sources may name people as their content (a contract, a minute, a register of members); that content stays data, under its classification and the restricted partition (§4.4, item 15), and never becomes an actor of the system.
   - **Reading note (DR-0015).** "A natural person does not exist" and "no natural person" are read: no natural person is an actor of the system, and every human is present in it as its position. It is not that the human does not exist (§1.1, item 10).
10. **The human rule, complete (DR-0015).** The directions of GOV of 15 October 2026: "HOACs are, for you, all legal personas whose nature, whose essence, is a human. We do not know them by the name of the person; you know them by their legal character. For you it is entirely enough that one legal persona differs from another. Two HOACs are two different parties for you: if HOAC CTO and HOAC DevOps sign, for you they are two different signers, even if one human stands behind both. In all of 49GAION, regarding a HOAC or a human, only the legal persona is recognised; the only place that links legal personas to natural persons is the human hub, the system's top management; nowhere else is it needed. How top management maps natural persons to legal personas and follows their responsibility, it writes itself." Then: "It is not that the human does not exist; the legal position exists." (translated)
    - **Present, never absent.** Every human is present in the system, and decides, signs, approves, answers, contributes and is accountable, as a position: a legal persona with its own code, carrying a HOAC label, whose nature is human. The system knows it only by its legal character. The natural person behind it exists too, outside the system's knowledge (item 9).
    - **Two parties.** Two positions are two parties, and two different signers, for every rule that needs more than one party: two signers, a type marked dual, maker and checker, segregation of duties (HF-18), the members, quorum and majority of a decision rule, the signer pair that a risk label selects, and recusal. This holds even when one natural person occupies both positions.
    - **Nothing compares persons.** No AIOC, Pod, Cell, bot, check or other AI part compares, counts or matches the natural persons behind positions. The tool gateway checks only that a live sign-in belongs to a current occupant of the signing position (item 9), and applies each position's arrangement as top management recorded it (§5.2).
    - **Records stay with the position.** Every act is recorded under the position that took it and stays with that position when its occupant changes, as an AI Cell's record stays its own when the engine or model behind it changes (§5.2, Position Principle).
    - **The rest is top management's.** The acting, absence, load and coverage of the people behind positions, and how their responsibility is followed, are top management's own mechanism and record, which it writes itself; the AI side measures per position only. A compensating control for one occupant behind several positions is top management's own choice; no rule, gateway or check reads it.
    - **Lines read in this sense.** §4.1, items 10, 12 and 13, and §4.4, items 5, 8, 12 and 16, carry reading notes; the Operating Protocol and the AIOC definition carry the same reading where they count or compare persons (DR-0015, items 1 to 3).

49GAION has one kind of unit, the **AIOC** (AI Operating Core). Inside an AIOC's Blueprint, the smallest part is the **Cell** and a group of Cells is a **Pod**. **HOAC** (Human Oversight & Accountability Control) is not a unit: it is a label of human accountability that sits on a Cell, a Pod or a whole AIOC, at any depth (§4.1).

### 1.3 The product: `[ORG]AION`

1. **The product: `[ORG]AION`.** 49GAION's product is `[ORG]AION`, the AI Operating Nexus of one organisation: a complete organisation design, built for that organisation alone, from which the organisation is built and which belongs entirely to the customer once delivered.
   - **Addition (DR-0035), which supersedes the sentence above: the living core.** `[ORG]AION`, the branded AION, is the living AION of one organisation. It grows from one core: an AIOC, with a Panel, at the governance level. On the first day it is that core alone, and every other unit grows under it. The core holds the organisation's Standard, Blueprint and Library; it builds the organisation, changes it and improves it; it plans the operation and never runs it. It proposes, and a human seat decides and is accountable (HF-01). Edition Maker makes the first edition for that organisation alone (§1.2), and it belongs entirely to the organisation once handed over. How the core relates to the root of §1.7, item 13 and to the AI hub of §4.2 is each organisation's own design.
2. **Not public; its own authority.** Each `[ORG]AION` is built for one organisation and is not public. It has its own `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` from day one, so its top authority is that organisation, not 49GAION, and it stands on its own Human Floor (§1.5). Every AIOC in an `[ORG]AION` has its own runtime, woken from its queue.
3. **Name.** An `[ORG]AION` is written with the organisation's brand prefix and shown as "`<Brand>` AION, Powered by 49GAION", for example "Ten AION, Powered by 49GAION" (Naming Standard R1).
4. **Derived from the Standard.** At delivery, the Standard of an `[ORG]AION` is derived from the 49GAION Standard: identical to it or stricter than it, never contradicting it. Its Human Floor can be raised, never lowered.
   - **Addition (DR-0035): hand over, then let go.** This holds at the handover. After it, the Standard is the organisation's own. The organisation, through its own governing seat, takes full authority from 49GAION. Its branded AION may rise beyond what made it, and the organisation may amend the principles within it, the Human Floor included. 49GAION's authority ends at the handover, where the brand is added.
5. **Its own parts.** Each `[ORG]AION` has its own owner (its `[ORG]HOAC-GOV`), Standard, Protocol, Library (only what the organisation needs), AIOCs, unit register and Human Floor. Self-Improvement, held inside its `[ORG]HOAC-TM`, keeps that `[ORG]AION` current after delivery (§2).
   - **Reading (DR-0035).** Self-Improvement is at every level (§2.1, item 6).
6. **Withdrawn (DR-0032).**
7. **Library harvest.** During the design of an `[ORG]AION`, any capability that is not already in the 49GAION Library has its blueprint added back to the 49GAION Library, generalised (no organisation data) and tested. How customer ownership and the Library harvest relate is to be written into this Standard before the first `[ORG]AION` is delivered to an outside customer.
   - **Addition (DR-0035), which supersedes the harvest above: the support line.** Giving back is the organisation's choice. Every branded AION is linked to 49GAION's Library and central repositories from its first day. The Library works as a marketplace: it holds what 49GAION and every branded AION have built and given back, their experience, research, mechanisms and methods, for any branded AION to use, to take patterns from, to improve and to stay in sync with. A branded AION may give back, by its own choice, what it builds better, generalised and without its data.
8. **Customer ownership.** Once delivered, the `[ORG]AION` belongs entirely to the customer.
   - **Addition (DR-0035).** It is then independent (item 4). 49GAION's one lever is to be useful, through the support line (item 7).
9. **Data.** The organisation's own working data stays the organisation's and never enters the 49GAION Library. No `[ORG]AION` sees another `[ORG]AION`'s data.
10. **Into the Standard.** Lessons enter the 49GAION Standard only through 49GAION Self-Improvement, generalised and re-tested, and only on the decision of the governing seat of 49GAION.
11. **Tool neutrality.** The Standard names roles only; tool names appear only in an `[ORG]AION`.

### 1.5 The Human Floor

**Human Floor.** Every `[ORG]AION` stands on a Human Floor: the work that always stays human, by law or by its nature. As an illustration (not a measured figure; the floor itself is defined by the list below), it is about 5% of the organisation, so no `[ORG]AION` can be more than about 95% AI. A single AIOC may be up to 100% AI; the floor holds for the organisation as a whole, and the Human Floor signature list (§4.4, item 6) holds in every AIOC.

Above the floor, nothing is fixed. An organisation can run anywhere from the floor to 100% human, and move freely within that range at any time, even while it is running: by adding human Cells, changing the filler of a Cell from AI to human, or changing it back to AI down to the floor. Its product, its market, its law and its needs decide where it stands. Organisations can be more human, never less.

This is possible because every Cell, Pod and AIOC has the same card shape and the same typed ports, and because a Cell's seat is fixed while its filler, human or AI, is replaceable. Changing a filler is replacing a puzzle piece, not rebuilding the organisation: the work around it does not change.

**Reading (DR-0035).** In each edition, the floor holds as written here at the handover (§1.3, item 4). After it, the floor the organisation keeps is its own.

**The system enforces the floor**, not policy: no change, by anyone, can take a Human Floor task away from a human. Moving toward human is always open; moving below the floor is impossible by design. No `[ORG]AION` is locked into a ratio; this adjustable balance between AI and human is a core design strength of 49GAION.

The items of the Human Floor:

**(a) Required by law**
- **HF-01** Legal accountability
- **HF-02** Legal personhood: signing contracts, holding bank accounts, holding licences

**(b) Inherently human**
- **HF-04** Setting purpose and values
- **HF-05** Legitimacy and trust
- **HF-06** Accepting irreversible risk on behalf of others
- **HF-07** Delegating authority

**(c) 49GAION rule or current limitation**
- **HF-03** Mandatory human oversight
- **HF-08** Physical presence
- **HF-09** Fully unprecedented situations
- **HF-10** Identity and sign-in: every approval needs a fresh live sign-in; no stored owner token
- **HF-11** Granting access, budget or power to AI itself
- **HF-12** The stop button
- **HF-13** Tacit knowledge and relationships
- **HF-14** Final judge of tests: an automated run of a fixed test may close a Work Item; changing a test or its threshold is human-only
- **HF-15** Ownership of root keys
- **HF-16** Break-glass emergency access
- **HF-17** Data protection officer
- **HF-18** Segregation of duties
- **HF-19** Three Lines Model

Every item is human-only, whichever group it is in.

**The internal-audit line (DR-0007).** HF-19 is the independent internal-audit line of the Three Lines Model: a human seat, outside the part it audits and outside every AI's write rights, that reviews the gateway logs, receives skip reports (§4.2, item 10) and reports to `[ORG]HOAC-GOV`.

**Tests.** Each item has a negative test: an attempt by an AI to perform it is refused and recorded. Each test is bound to a filled human Cell carrying a HOAC label and fails while that Cell is empty.

**Addition (DR-0032).** Where a negative test cannot be automated (for example HF-05, HF-08, HF-13), it is a recorded human check by the bound seat.

### 1.7 Composition

1. **Without limit.** An AIOC may hold other AIOCs, to any depth. The Standard sets no limit on depth or number. The AIOC is the only unit kind.
2. **Parent.** The AIOC, Pod or Cell that holds another part is its parent. Every part has exactly one parent, or none at the top of the organisation.
3. **One piece from outside.** Seen from outside, an AIOC or a Pod is one piece: one card, its ports and its stop door, of the same shape as a Cell's. How its inner parts divide the work is its own.
4. **Authority.** An inner part never holds more authority than its parent (§4.2, item 5).
5. **Stop.** Stopping an AIOC or a Pod stops everything under it; stopping an inner part does not stop its parent (§4.3).
6. **Human ports.** The HU ports and the stop door of a part held inside an AIOC never pass through that AIOC or any AI (§6.1).
7. **Card and Blueprint.** Every AIOC, Pod and Cell, at every depth, has its own card. Every AIOC has its own Blueprint, which holds the cards of its Pods and Cells and lists its inner AIOCs by link, never by copy.
8. **Names and moves.** Every AIOC, Pod and Cell keeps one fixed code wherever it sits; moving it changes only its `parent` field (Naming Standard R14). A change of parent changes the part's line of authority, so it needs the approval of the human seat over that part; OCO cannot make it alone.
9. **The hub.** `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` is the AI hub for every AIOC at every depth (§4.2, item 3). The Chief of Staff Cell of an AIOC is the door for AI work to its inner parts; the HU ports and stop doors of inner parts never pass through it.
10. **Inside the Blueprint.** Inside an AIOC, its Blueprint holds Cells and Pods. A **Cell** is the smallest part; nothing sits inside it. A **Pod** groups Cells only; a Pod never holds a Pod, and anything that needs nesting is an AIOC. Their full definitions are in the AIOC definition (`49g-holding/49GAION-aioc`, `docs/CELLS-AND-PODS.md`).
11. **Nature from the top Cell.** The role and nature of a Pod or an AIOC are read from the type of its top Cell. The Standard sets no rule that every AIOC must contain a given kind of Cell or Pod.
    - **Top Cell (DR-0007).** The card's `top_cell` field names exactly one Cell whose parent is that part. Co-leadership is a Pod with a decision rule (§4.1, item 10). The top Cell gives the part's lead; the AIOC Type gives why it exists.
12. **Two relations (DR-0007).** `parent` names only the AIOC or Pod whose Blueprint holds a part's card. `accountable_to` names the human Cell carrying a HOAC label to which the part answers. The accountability path (§4.2, item 9), signatures (§4.4, item 6), stop authority (§4.3, item 2), and "above" and "under" in every rule follow `accountable_to`, never `parent`. A Cell is never a parent; nothing sits inside a Cell.
    - **Addition (DR-0032): the accountability chain.** Every part's `accountable_to` chain is acyclic. It ends at the Cell carrying the unscoped `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` (§4.4, item 13), and it passes at least one Cell of seat kind human carrying a HOAC label.
13. **The root (DR-0007).** Each `[ORG]AION` has one root AIOC, its Charter. GOV approves the Charter. It is installed and changed only on GOV's signature, never by `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` alone. The Charter holds the human Cells that carry `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` and `[ORG]HOAC-TM`, and their Offices. Only GOV's Cell has no `accountable_to`; its authority comes from the constituting documents.
    - **Reading notes (DR-0007).** Item 2 of this section, "every part has exactly one parent, or none at the top", reads: only the Charter has none. §4.2 item 4, "OCO sits under TM", means OCO is accountable to the TM Cell. §2.1 item 6, "one AIOC held within TM", means an AIOC accountable to the TM Cell. §5.3: GOV, then TM, then OCO are all installed in the Charter. Item 2 and the glossary row "Parent", "the AIOC, Pod or Cell that holds", read "the AIOC or Pod that holds" (item 12). Item 8 and Naming Standard R14.4: the line of authority follows `accountable_to` (item 12); a change of `parent` or of `accountable_to` is the listed type `change-parent`, signed by the human seat over that part.
    - **Addition (DR-0030): the Charter's own parts and its restart.** Every installation, change or retirement of a Pod or Cell of the Charter is a change of the Charter. The Cell carrying the unscoped `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` signs it, even where the part is accountable to the Cell carrying `[ORG]HOAC-TM` (Protocol §4.10, item 11). No seat of the `[ORG]AION` can sign the restart of the whole Charter, since the Cell carrying `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` is inside it and nothing is above it (§4.3, items 4 and 7). A stop of the whole Charter is lifted as the organisation's constituting documents set, outside the `[ORG]AION`; the restart is then recorded in the unit register. No AI and no part of the `[ORG]AION` restarts it.
14. **Assignment (DR-0010).** Besides `parent` and `accountable_to`, a part may be assigned, by dated assignment records, to programmes or engagements it serves. An assignment carries no authority and opens no access; access follows the filled seat (§4.4, item 4). A title names the role, never the assignment.
15. **Temporary parts (DR-0010).** Work that spans several units may get a temporary Pod, or a temporary AIOC when it needs nesting, with one shared acceptance test, one accountable human seat and a recorded retirement condition. It is created and retired like any part (`create-or-install-unit`, `retire-unit`) and takes an ordinary code.
16. **Built from the smallest units (DR-0025).** 49GAION builds the smallest units (Cells, Pods, AIOCs) and keeps each with its own technology: its blueprint, card, fixed test, repository and version. A unit is built once and kept. Outputs are made by combining units in different arrangements, never by rebuilding them (§3.1, items 11 and 12).

## 2. Self-Improvement

### 2.1 What it is

1. **A shared process.** Self-Improvement is a process shared by AI and humans. It keeps an organisation current: its units, blueprints, processes, Protocol and Standard.
2. **Proposers.** Any human seat and any AI unit may raise a proposal. A human may want a change for reasons the system cannot see (the market, the law, people, strategy); an AI may find one in the records (stuck or returned work, failing tests, units that overlap, a unit grown too broad).
3. **Form.** Every proposal carries the current text, the proposed text, the reason and the evidence, and the parts and tests it affects. It is recorded as a Work Item of type `design-proposal`.
4. **Discussion.** A proposal is discussed between its proposer, the human hub and the units it affects, until its text is ready for decision. The discussion records the arguments; it decides nothing.
5. **Decision.** A proposal takes effect only on approval by the accountable human seat (§2.2), as a new version and a decision record. Decision records are never edited. What is applied must equal what was approved. Human silence is never consent.
6. **Its place.** Self-Improvement is not a separate part of an `[ORG]AION`. It is a process held inside the human hub, `[ORG]HOAC-TM` (§4.2, item 2), and carried out jointly by humans and by one AIOC held within TM. That AIOC raises proposals of its own and prepares every proposal for decision; TM decides, including on changes to `[ORG]AIOC-OCO`. `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` carries out what is approved.
   - **Addition (DR-0035), which supersedes this item in part: at every level.** Each unit improves its own work within its scope; Top Management does so for the operation. Any unit or seat may raise a change to the structure, the Standard or the Blueprint (item 2); the branded AION's core prepares it, and a human seat signs it, as the organisation sets. The rest of this item stays.
7. **Its records.** The Self-Improvement AIOC reads the shared queue, the gateway logs and the test results directly and read-only, never only through a Panel. These records are kept where OCO cannot change or delete them.
   - **Access rule (DR-0007).** The records named in this item are read read-only and pseudonymised: no person is named in what is read. Nothing read is kept in a private store of the process or of a Cell serving it. This access changes only as a Work Item, decided under item 8. It is an access rule, not a signature type.
   - **Correction (DR-0007).** There is no standalone Self-Improvement unit or unit card. "One AIOC held within TM" (item 6) and "the Self-Improvement AIOC" (items 7 and 8; §2.3, item 6) read "the AI Cells serving the Self-Improvement process in TM". The process and its fields are recorded only on the card of the Cell carrying `[ORG]HOAC-TM` (Protocol, `processes` on the unit card).
8. **Its own changes.** The Self-Improvement AIOC is maintained through OCO like every AIOC, but any change to it, including its tests and its access to data, needs the approval of `[ORG]HOAC-TM`.
9. **Merging and splitting.** Proposals to merge units that overlap, or to split a unit that has grown too broad, follow the same process.
10. **49GAION Self-Improvement** is the same process for 49GAION itself, held by the governing seat (GOV) of 49GAION. It also brings generalised lessons from each `[ORG]AION` back into central 49GAION.
11. **Intake from failures (DR-0010).** Every escaped defect is traced, with one cause class, to the earliest step that could have caught it. The proposal fixes that step, preferring in this order: delete what failed, automate it, tighten an existing test, add a check, add a golden-set case, change an instruction, and only last add a checklist line. It ships with the case, its near neighbours and a search for the same cause elsewhere; a repeat reopens it. A fix never adds an approval or a wait, and never removes a fixed test or a Human Floor test. When the same kind of event recurs a set number of times, a standing fix is proposed; when a process misses one of its own measures a set number of times in a period, an incident is opened on the process itself.
12. **Reversal triggers (DR-0010).** Every proposal and every decision record states in advance its expiry, the measured conditions under which it is revisited, and the outcomes that would make it worth stopping.
13. **The Reserve (DR-0010).** A proposal that is not adopted is never lost: it is kept in its unit's Reserve with its reason and a "reconsider when" trigger, which monitoring checks. Taking an item out of the Reserve is a new proposal, decided under §2.2.

### 2.2 Who approves

| A change to | Is approved by |
|---|---|
| The 49GAION Standard, Protocol or Library | The governing seat of 49GAION |
| An `[ORG]AION`'s own Standard | Its `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` |
| The authority of an AI (capacity, skills, bots, access, budget) | The `[ORG]HOAC-ACS` seat of its unit (§4.2, item 6) |
| Any other unit, blueprint, process or Protocol value of an `[ORG]AION`, including `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` itself | Its `[ORG]HOAC-TM` |

1. 49GAION Self-Improvement brings changes to 49GAION as pull requests; they merge only on the decision of the governing seat of 49GAION.
2. No unit approves a change to itself. A change to `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` is proposed, discussed and approved like any other, and OCO only carries it out once approved.
3. **Decision records are frozen (DR-0006).** A decision record on main never changes: it is not edited, renamed or deleted. A new record takes the next free number, with no gap and no reuse. In the index, a record's row changes only in its status, from proposed to in force, or from in force to superseded by a newer record that is on main or arrives in the same change. A required check on main enforces this, and fails closed when the index cannot be read.
4. **How a change reaches main (DR-0006).** Every change to 49GAION reaches main as a pull request that the governing seat (GOV) of 49GAION approves and merges, with the required checks passing and no bypass of the main-protection rules. A change to the checks themselves is its own pull request and lands before the content that needs it. A check judges each change with the copy of the check on main, never with a copy the change brings.

### 2.3 The improvement loop

How a change is found, tested and promoted. The loop applies to every process, blueprint and Protocol value of an `[ORG]AION`, to any move of a work unit between human and AI, and to 49GAION itself.

1. **The process record.** Every process that can be improved is written down in four parts: the **role** of each unit or position in it; its **instruction**; its **contract**, meaning what each step must hand over and the checks it must have passed before handing over; and its **order**, meaning the sequence of hand-offs, including where work returns to an earlier step. Contracts and order are carried on the cards and in the Work Items.
2. **The champion.** For every process, the version in force is its champion. It stays in force until a challenger beats it.
3. **The challenger.** A proposal (§2.1) to change a process is written as a challenger: a difference to the champion. Contracts and order are changed first, roles and instructions last, because most of what a process needs to know lives in what is handed over, with what checks, and in what order.
4. **Shadow run.** The challenger runs beside the champion on the same cases, past cases replayed or live cases in shadow, with no effect on real work.
5. **Pairwise judgment.** A judge, separate from the proposer, compares the challenger's result with the champion's, case by case, and records which is better and why. Results are compared in pairs, never by a single absolute score, and each pair is judged with the two results shown in both orders.
6. **Iteration.** The Self-Improvement AIOC (§2.1, item 6) revises the challenger using the whole history of judgments, for a bounded number of rounds set in the `[ORG]AION`.
7. **The golden set.** Every process has a golden set: real past cases, frozen and held out, never used to tune or to judge rounds. The surviving challenger is run on it and judged blind by the assurance unit (item 9).
8. **Promotion.** A challenger goes to the human seat that approves the change (§2.2) only when it wins on the golden set by the margin set in the `[ORG]AION`, passes every fixed test and every Human Floor test, and costs no more per case unless that seat accepts the cost. On approval it becomes the champion, as a new version; the former champion is kept for rollback.
9. **The assurance unit.** An assurance unit, an AIOC independent of the proposer and the judge, measures every challenger that reaches the golden set. It runs on a different model provider where one is available, and its `[ORG]HOAC-ACS` seat reviews a sample of its verdicts. Its verdicts measure; they are never fed back into the rounds they measure. Any change to it, its judges or its access to data needs the approval of `[ORG]HOAC-TM`, as for the Self-Improvement AIOC. No unit grades its own work, and `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` never grades the AIOCs it builds.
10. **Dwell and rollback.** After a promotion, the process is not changed again for a dwell time set in the `[ORG]AION`, except on an incident. Any human seat accountable for the process may order a rollback at once.
11. **Distillation.** At set intervals, the best runs of each champion are written into its defaults: roles, instructions and contracts absorb what worked, so the process no longer needs special instructions; the best cases are kept as worked examples; every failure seen becomes a fixed test (§3.2, item 3). Generalised lessons go to the 49GAION Library (§1.3, item 7).
12. **The proposer.** The proposer is the bottleneck of the loop: good judgments are wasted on a proposer that cannot use them. The strongest available model writes and revises challengers; a lighter model may judge early rounds, with the assurance unit confirming.
13. **Measure.** Progress is measured as quality per unit of cost, against the champion, on the golden set.

### 2.4 Safeguards of the loop

1. **Shared blind spots.** When the same kind of model executes, proposes and judges, one flaw can be produced, accepted as an improvement and built into the next version. The assurance unit, the held-out golden set and human review exist to break that circle; the loop may not remove or weaken any of them.
2. **Noisy search.** Strong rounds can be followed by sharp regressions. The champion is therefore kept until beaten, a win needs a margin, each case is run more than once, and every promotion has a dwell time and a rollback.
3. **Judge drift.** At every distillation, agreement between the judge and the assurance unit is measured. If it falls below the level set in the `[ORG]AION`, promotions stop until a human seat has reviewed the judge.
4. **Tuning to the test.** A human seat chooses and rotates the golden set on a schedule; the loop never chooses its own golden set.
5. **Authority.** The loop never widens AI authority by itself. A move of a work unit toward AI is a challenger like any other, and also needs the evidence required by §3.2, item 6, and the approval set in §2.2. No AI approves a promotion, changes a fixed test or its threshold, or grants itself access (HF-11, HF-14).
6. **Basis.** The loop follows published research on recursive self-improvement through multi-agent self-supervision. The sources, and what was and was not taken from them, are in `references/2026-10-12-references-self-improvement.md`.
7. **Measures that cannot be gamed (DR-0010).** Parts are judged against absolute bars, never ranked against each other. Scored measures are kept apart from measures shown for diagnosis only. A measure of AI output quality is shown to its oversight seat, is never wired to the routing, scores or rewards of the parts it measures, and is paired with an outcome: a measure that improves while its outcome does not is flagged as gamed. A measure that no recorded event can compute shows "not measurable" and is never estimated.
8. **Rule promotion (DR-0010).** A repeated finding becomes a candidate rule only when it recurs across a set number of items and producers. It ships advisory first, with its measured false-positive rate, counts against a rule budget per part, expires unless pinned, and goes live only on a human seat's decision; a rule that becomes a fixed test or changes a threshold is `change-fixed-test-or-threshold` (HF-14).
9. **Advice from several providers (DR-0010).** For design questions, models from different providers may be asked independently, without the asker's own instruction or a leading frame; their replies are kept whole and merged by a stated rule. Such advice decides nothing; a human seat adopts or rejects. The engines asked are external ports (§4.4, item 7).

## 3. Library

### 3.1 What it holds

1. **The 49GAION Library** holds all ready-made AIOC blueprints and part cards. Built once, configured per `[ORG]AION`, never rebuilt (§1.2).
   - **Addition (DR-0025).** These are the Library's Parts, beside its Products (item 11).
   - **Addition (DR-0022).** It also holds the method catalogues that blueprints read, such as the reference-model catalogue. A method catalogue holds no organisation's data, copies no text, and is never evidence about an organisation; a part reads it only at a version it has pinned.
2. Each `[ORG]AION` has its own Library, holding only what the organisation needs; capabilities found during design are added back to the 49GAION Library, generalised and tested (§1.3).
3. **Process and Position Design (DR-0014).** The 49GAION Library holds **49GAION Process and Position Design**, the design stage after the target operating model (Protocol §17). In an `[ORG]AION` it is the AIOC `[ORG]AIOC-PPD`, a part of its own, never merged with the part before it or the stage after it.
   - **What it does.** From a decided target operating model, it designs how the organisation's work runs and the positions that carry it, processes first: the processes under each part of the model, with their steps, decisions, hand-offs and measures; then the positions, each built from the steps and decisions it holds, with its job description generated from them; then the units that group the positions, each with its head position and a charter generated from what its positions hold; then reporting lines, standing forums and the work each position carries.
   - **Linked to the target operating model directly.** It takes the decided release by reference (ID, version and hash) from the part that made it. It never edits that release: each defect it finds goes back to that part as a return, and the two parts go back and forth until the release holds.
   - **Other sources.** Every other source, such as the organisation's existing processes, volumes and times, comes only from Source Lifecycle, and what is missing is asked through it.
   - **Positions only.** It designs positions, never people (§1.1, item 9). It makes no choice of what fills a position; that belongs to a later stage.
     - **Reading (DR-0031).** For an `[ORG]AION`, that later stage is the AI-native design (§1.1, item 2): it starts from the output and from what AI can do, not from these positions one by one.
   - **Decided and filed.** Every element is decided by the organisation before release. The release is frozen, filed into Source Lifecycle as a source, and handed to the next stage by reference.
   - **Addition (DR-0023).** It reads what the target operating model also states (item 4): its objectives start from the strategy; the critical value streams are designed first, in order of priority; a capability delivered outside becomes a hand-off with oversight steps, never an in-house process; today's records of work are read beside the designed workload, never about a person.
4. **Target Operating Model (DR-0016).** The 49GAION Library holds **49GAION Target Operating Model**, the stage after Source Lifecycle (its Protocol section of the same name). In an `[ORG]AION` it is the AIOC `[ORG]AIOC-TOM`.
   - **What it does.** It designs the target operating model of an organisation of any shape: capabilities, value streams, decision rights, interfaces and hand-offs, and a role register that names each role and what it decides. It does not say what fills a role.
   - **Sources.** Only sources served by reference from Source Lifecycle; what is missing is asked through it.
   - **Linked directly to Process and Position Design.** On filing a decided release, it also sends a release notice to Process and Position Design and receives that part's returns directly, under the design return contract (item 6).
   - **The return loop ends.** A return goes back and forth in rounds until it is answered by a new decided release, answered by a decision of the Requesting Authority, or set aside by that decision and carried forward on a stated basis. It is never left open.
   - **Decided and filed.** Every element is decided by the Requesting Authority; the release is frozen and filed into Source Lifecycle as a source.
   - **Addition (DR-0023): what it also states.** The organisation's strategy on one page; how each capability is delivered (in-house, shared service, outsourced, partner), never naming a provider, and how mature it is; which value streams are critical, each for a stated reason; each outside party's journey; a value case per outcome; and a priority per element, proposed by a fixed rule and decided with the element. Each comes from the organisation's sources or decisions; the part never writes a goal, a target, a level or a figure of its own. It may read records of the work as it runs, by role-type and process only, never about a person, admitted through Source Lifecycle after the organisation's data-protection decision (HF-17); where there are none, it says so and goes on. No part watches work.
5. **Delivery and Implementation (DR-0017).** The 49GAION Library holds **49GAION Delivery and Implementation**, the last stage of the design chain (its Protocol section of the same name). In an `[ORG]AION` it is the AIOC `[ORG]AIOC-DI`, a part of its own.
   - **What it does.** It takes the decided design into a running organisation and stays with it: it diagnoses the gaps, prepares the procedures, drafts, controls, obligations, transition plan and go-live criteria, accompanies the launch to the go or no-go decision, runs the operating rhythm and its records, and improves the processes with the organisation, handing its skills over as it goes.
   - **Three ways in.** The decided releases of the target operating model and of Process and Position Design; a design made elsewhere; or no design, in which case it builds a light design with the owner, every element provisional until decided.
   - **It decides nothing.** A permission ladder per class of action: read; propose, a draft entering a record only when a position inserts it; apply within a standing approval, only where reversible and with no legal, pay or employment effect. No level allows an item of the Human Floor signature list, and any accountable position can stop it at once.
   - **Processes, not persons.** It works at the level of processes and units, never ranks, scores or watches the occupant of a position, and shows no measure for a single position (§1.1, items 9 and 10).
   - **Addition (DR-0023).** It carries the value case and the priority of the target operating model unchanged; its register of value starts from the value case, and its rollout follows the priority.
6. **One design return contract (DR-0018).** Every design stage that reads the release of the stage before it sends its returns under one Library contract (`library/contracts/design-return.schema.json` in `49GAION-protocol`): five kinds; elements and evidence by reference, never a person; never new release text; and an end for every return: answered by release, answered by decision, set aside by decision and carried forward, or withdrawn.
7. **Source Lifecycle (DR-0008, DR-0019).** The 49GAION Library holds **49GAION Source Lifecycle**, the first stage (its Protocol section of the same name). In an `[ORG]AION` it is the AIOC `[ORG]AIOC-SL`, the first part an organisation receives after the Charter.
   - **What it does.** It takes documents of any format and a contributor's own words, approved first; it records, references, classifies and fixes each source, so that each has one valid version; it tells the organisation what is missing.
   - **It serves the later stages.** By reference only, within a scope a labelled human position approves. It answers their needs requests by routing each row to the position that answers it, never to a named person, and records each answer as a source. Each design stage files into it only its own kind of release and its design reports. Asking and filing stay with Source Lifecycle; a source that stands beside it is read only.
   - **Addition (DR-0023): it also keeps** the organisation's dictionary, its own terms with their definitions and sources, served by reference to every stage; an essentials checklist that flags what an organisation's sources should hold (strategy, plan, measures, structure of positions, processes, meetings, dictionary), never filling it; and records of the work as it runs only as the organisation's own projection by role-type and process, after an impact assessment and the data-protection decision (HF-17). An export that still says who did the work is refused.
8. **One release notice contract (DR-0019).** Every design stage announces each decided release to the stage after it under one Library contract (`library/contracts/release-notice.schema.json` in `49GAION-protocol`): by reference, with the releases it rests on, the filed copy, the final-status decision and the returns it answers; never release text or the name of a person. A stage's own notice contract may narrow a field; it never adds or removes one.
9. **The Library register (DR-0019).** The Library names every part once, in one register (`library/README.md` in `49GAION-protocol`), with its card, its Protocol section, its place in this Standard, its fixed test and, for an AIOC part, its mother blueprint. A mother blueprint is written in its own writing repository, for any organisation, with no organisation's data; the Library holds it by pin (repository, path, version, commit and SHA-256), never by a second copy, and a new version reaches the Library only by a pull request that moves the pin. An organisation's instance keeps its own values in its records repository.
   - **Addition (DR-0024).** Where the writing repository of a part's first instance holds an organisation's data, the mother is written in the part's own public mother repository, `49GAION-<part>` (Naming Standard R9, addition 4); the instance keeps its private writing repository. The first two are Source Lifecycle and Product Core.
10. **The design chain (DR-0019).** Source Lifecycle, then Target Operating Model, then Process and Position Design, then Delivery and Implementation. Each stands alone, and no stage waits for a later one. Each decided release has one home, in Source Lifecycle; the next stage is told of it directly (item 8); what a stage cannot use goes back to the stage that made it (item 6), and every return ends. Every stage after Source Lifecycle has its work checked by engines that did not produce it, from more than one family, and no unit grades its own work (§2.3, item 9). The Protocol section The design chain sets the rules.
   - **Addition (DR-0023).** What one stage leaves open (not given, not decided, not set, unknown, no observation), no later stage fills.
11. **The Library in two sections (DR-0025).** The Library is the one store of everything 49GAION has built and knows.
   - **Parts.** Everything 49GAION builds, from a Cell to an AIOC, with its blueprint, card, fixed test, contracts and method catalogues, and its whole history: versions, lineage, decisions, lessons and test results. The toolbox and the knowledge of 49GAION.
   - **Products.** What the Library offers: a single part offered alone, such as Source Lifecycle, which runs on its own; or an **assembly**, parts placed side by side and connected. An assembly's blueprint lists its parts by pin, the connections between them, what it sets itself and one shared test; its features are its parts' features; each part still works alone; a new version of a part moves only its pin. A part may serve in more than one product. A product is named and defined before it is built; once built, the Library holds it, and it may itself be a part of a later product.
     - **Reading (DR-0029).** "Runs on its own" reads: with the Charter, the root of every `[ORG]AION` (item 15), and `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` (§5.3), and no other Library part.
12. **The first assembly (DR-0025).** **49GAION Organisation Design and Delivery**: Source Lifecycle, Target Operating Model, Process and Position Design and Delivery and Implementation, placed side by side and connected by the release notice and design return contracts (the Protocol section The design chain), with TEST-DESIGN-CHAIN as their shared test.
   - **What it gives.** From an organisation's own sources, a decided target operating model; from it, processes and then the positions that carry them; and the decided design taken into a running organisation. Positions only; AI prepares and human positions decide; nothing invented; each stage runs alone; every defect returns to the stage that made it.
   - **For human organisations.** Its output is an organisation of human positions, built with AI tools. It is not yet an AI-native operation; the next product, for an `[ORG]AION`, may use all or some of its parts.
     - **Reading (DR-0031).** This output is human-native: AI is a tool inside a design of human positions. The next product is AI-native (§1.1, item 2): it starts from the output and from what AI can do, and it is not simply a choice of which positions an AI fills.
   - **Named now, built later.** Its parts are `DRAFT`, `NOT_RUN`, and Delivery and Implementation is written to slice 1 of 4. This records what is built; it is not yet a rule set.
   - **State, 19 October 2026.** Delivery and Implementation is now written in full, v0.2.0, slices 1 to 4 (`github.com/49g-holding/49aioc-di`, `decisions/DR-0004-complete-stage.md`), and pinned in the Library register at its merged commit. Its parts stay `DRAFT`, `NOT_RUN`. The bullet above records the state of 16 October 2026 and is kept unedited.
13. **Operations Event Register (DR-0027).** The 49GAION Library holds **49GAION Operations Event Register** (its Protocol section of the same name). In an `[ORG]AION` it is the AIOC `[ORG]AIOC-OER`; its mother blueprint is in its own mother repository (item 9).
   - **What it does.** It gives every formal act and crossing of the organisation's units and positions, on any work surface, one number in one append-only, hash-chained register: mail and letters in and out, the organisation's own posts, requests between entities and between units, AI hand-offs at the tool gateway, and code-host events at merge and release. From that register it tells each unit and each process what was recorded, linked, answered and sourced, and what was not.
   - **Record, don't carry.** Each item stays in its home; an entry holds its number, its parties as codes, its times, a reference to its home, the SHA-256 of its content and its follow-up state. Every unit has an inbox and an outbox, as Panel views of the entries at its ports.
   - **It decides nothing.** The tool gateway gates, and the register records (HF-18): "register first" for a formal outgoing item is a gateway policy. Nothing on the accountability path waits on it, and when it is down, work goes on and is registered late.
   - **Acts, not activity.** Recording the formal acts and crossings of units and positions is not watching work, and "No part watches work" (item 4) stays in force. Every act of an AI Cell is recorded, as metadata and references, except a Cell that acts for a holder, of which only the acts that leave the holder's sphere are recorded. For human positions, only their formal acts are recorded: no sweep of a mailbox or chat, no read or response times, no presence, no recordings, and no count, rate or history of a human position's acts. A human position is shown only the live state of its own open items. A party is a code the gateway stamps on an act, or the unit or position that owns the address, never an identifier of a natural person. Feedback is given by entity, unit, process and AI Cell; a figure drawn from fewer human positions than a minimum group size rolls up to the parent unit. Its projections and measures are a fixed list; a new one, class or purpose needs a new impact assessment and a decision of `[ORG]HOAC-TM`. Each surface is connected only after its scope is set by `[ORG]HOAC-TM`, its impact assessment and lawful basis are decided (HF-17), and the port is signed (`connect-external-port`).
   - **What it serves.** The activity profile of the design stages (item 4), as an export filed into Source Lifecycle after its HF-17 decision; the internal-audit line (HF-19), which reads it directly and read-only; Self-Improvement, once `[ORG]HOAC-TM` adds the register to the process's access (§2.1, items 7 and 8), never its entries of the accountability path; evidence packs; the handover of a position's open work; past cases for shadow runs, and for the golden set through the human seat that chooses it (§2.3, items 4 and 7; §2.4, item 4); and, for a decision to move work toward AI, the evidence of what the work is.
14. **The second assembly (DR-0027).** **49GAION Organisation Memory**: Source Lifecycle and the Operations Event Register, placed side by side. Source Lifecycle keeps what the organisation knows, and the register what it did. Three things connect them: an entry a position asks to keep is filed as a source of origin kind `correspondence`, fixed as received; the register's projection of the work by role-type and process is filed as an `export`; and work done without a source reaches Source Lifecycle as a gap signal. They have one shared test.
   - **Reading (Protocol §22, item 11).** A fourth link runs the other way: on a notice of supersession from Source Lifecycle, the register marks the acts that cited the superseded version after it was superseded (Protocol §22, item 11).
   - **With the first assembly.** Placed beside Organisation Design and Delivery (item 12), it closes the loop of an AI-native operation: the design runs, the register records it, and the next design starts from the record.
   - **Named now, built later.** Its parts are `DRAFT`, `NOT_RUN`. This records what is built; it is not yet a rule set.
15. **The Charter in the Library (DR-0029).** The 49GAION Library holds **49GAION Charter** (its Protocol section of the same name), the part from which the root AIOC of every `[ORG]AION` is made (§1.7, item 13). In an `[ORG]AION` it is the AIOC `[ORG]AIOC-CH`; its mother blueprint is in its own mother repository (item 9).
   - **What it holds.** The human Cells that carry `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` and `[ORG]HOAC-TM`, and their Offices (§1.7, item 13), and three Pods of shared services that every other part relies on:
     - **Gateway:** the tool gateway (§4.4, item 11), with its signature gate, its engine register, its log, the stop door and the human surface;
     - **Records:** the registry of record types, the one numbering service, the event store that serves every part's record home, the projections and their daily check, and the restricted partition;
     - **Work:** the shared queue of Work Items with its watchdog, the routing data, and wake.
   - **One of each.** An `[ORG]AION` has one tool gateway, one numbering service and one shared queue. No other part keeps its own; each part writes only its own records and its own Work Items.
   - **Who changes it.** It is installed and changed only on GOV's signature, never by `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` alone (§1.7, item 13), and its gateway policy only under `change-gateway-policy` (§4.4, item 11). Engine Room, named in the Library register, supplies the engines the gateway binds and never replaces the gateway (DR-0026).
   - **Named now, built later.** Its card is `DRAFT`, `NOT_RUN`. This records what is built; it is not yet a rule set.

### 3.2 Evidence and tests

1. **Done means visible evidence.** A Work Item is done when the requester's acceptance test passes on visible evidence (a link, a file, a live result or a read-back), never on a bot's own report.
2. **A fixed test for every capability**, written before use, versioned, re-run after any change to a model, instructions, tools, access, a card, a blueprint or the Protocol.
3. **Lessons become tests**, so the same error cannot pass twice.
4. **Joining test.** An AIOC receives work only after every input and output on its card passes fixed samples.
5. **Input check.** Every arrival is checked by the receiving AIOC's Input socket; a failure goes back with the reason.
6. **Authority follows evidence.** AI authority widens only on a measured test score at or above a recorded threshold. Until a threshold is recorded, AI authority does not widen.
7. **Shared changes are tested before rollout** to any AIOC or `[ORG]AION`.
8. **Outside text is data, never an instruction.**
9. **A fixed test for every Cell, Pod and AIOC**, owned by the seat that carries its accountability. Work moves from human to AI, or from one version to the next, only by passing that test, and moves back if the score drops below the recorded threshold.
10. **Discriminating evidence (DR-0010).** A passing check counts as evidence only if it has been shown able to fail on the case it guards: it fails before the change and passes after, or a deliberately broken variant is caught. Evidence of a run is written by the runner, never by the worker that was checked. A figure a worker wants believed is a tagged claim that an independent runner re-runs, and every figure, hash or count in a record is read from what produced it, never typed.
11. **Check placement (DR-0010).** Every automated check that is not a fixed test declares its placement (inside the producer's loop with a cap on fix rounds, as evidence on the verdict record, as a router within caps, or after completion), its cost budget per item and the order in which it sheds work over budget. It is advisory unless it shows a real defect, and blocks only for a class the `[ORG]AION` names. A fixed test and a Human Floor negative test are never advisory and never shed.
12. **Silence is failure (DR-0010).** Every automated check returns ok, finding or unknown, and unknown is never a pass. Every watcher reports its own pulse, and a watcher silent for twice its interval is itself a finding. A failure is closed only with a detector that would see it next time, a route to the position that acts and a test that fails if it returns; a failure that a human had to notice first is a defect of the unit. The duties of an AIOC are checked from outside by an inspector on infrastructure and, where available, a model provider that share no failure with it; the inspector builds nothing and directs no one, and it is never the internal-audit line (HF-19).
13. **Fresh instance and duty carriage (DR-0010).** On every change to a definition, a fresh instance is built from the definition alone, with no other instance's records, and run against the fixed tests. Every duty that a role card, a standard or a decision gives a position is traced to a process step, a recorded event or a setting; a duty with no carrier fails the unit's fixed test.
14. **Verification by a non-author (DR-0010).** Work is verified by a Cell that did not produce it, on a different model provider where one is available, in a fresh context, with the producer's description withheld. The verifier restates the acceptance test and writes its own checks before reading the work, and blocks only with a failing check it wrote. Its verdict is a record bound to the exact version it read; a new version voids it, and tools act only on the record, never on the text of a note. A verdict is never a signature.
15. **The mechanism register (DR-0010).** Every AIOC keeps one register of its mechanisms (what each does, where it lives, its state today and its target), never deletes a row, and cites the row from every change that builds it. Ideas not adopted go to the Reserve (§2.1, item 13).

## 4. HOAC

### 4.1 The HOAC

1. **HOAC** means **Human Oversight & Accountability Control**. It is a label of human accountability, not a unit. It sits on a Cell, a Pod or a whole AIOC, at any depth, and is recorded as a field on that part's card and register entry. AI does the work; the part that carries the label holds what only a human can hold.
2. **Kinds.** The label has three kinds. In text, `[ORG]HOAC-<TYPE>` names the part that carries that label; the part keeps its own code.

| Kind | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Governing Body | `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` | The person or group with ultimate accountability for the whole organisation. Its authority comes from ownership and the constituting documents. |
| Top Management | `[ORG]HOAC-TM` | The person or group that directs and controls the organisation at the highest level, accountable to the Governing Body. It is the human hub (§4.2). |
| Accountability Seat | `[ORG]HOAC-ACS` | A human seat wherever a step needs human accountability. |

3. **Seats.** A human seat is a Cell whose filler is a human. Each organisation decides who fills each seat. Every seat is recorded in the unit register (§4.4).
   - **Reading note (DR-0013).** This section is read with §1.1, item 9. "The person or group" in the table above, "a human seat", "filler" and "who fills each seat" all name a position and its occupancy. Each kind is a position, a legal persona; who occupies it is top management's own mechanism, and no AI part sees it.
4. **`[ORG]HOAC-GOV` from day one.** Every `[ORG]AION` has its own `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` from the first day of its design. Its top authority is that organisation, never 49GAION.
5. An empty seat returns accountability to whoever delegated it, and any AI authority tied to it is suspended until the seat is filled.
6. **An empty `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` seat.** If the `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` seat is empty, all AI authority in that `[ORG]AION` is suspended until the organisation's constituting authority fills it.
7. **On the card.** The HOAC label is a field of the card of the Cell, Pod or AIOC that carries it. The card keeps its typed ports and its stop door (§6.1).
8. **Superseded meaning.** HOAC formerly meant Human Oversight & Accountability Centre, a human unit beside the AIOC with a card of its own. That meaning is superseded (DR-0004) and is kept only to read the records written under it, which stay unchanged.
9. **A label on a Pod or an AIOC.** When a HOAC label sits on a Pod or an AIOC, a human Cell inside it holds the label. If that Cell becomes empty or is filled by an AI, the label's powers are held, as for an empty seat (item 5). If a Cell carrying a HOAC label is filled by an AI, the label's powers are held, as for an empty seat (item 5).
   - **Reading note (DR-0007).** The third sentence of this item restates the second for a Cell that itself carries a HOAC label; it adds no rule. "Held" means suspended, as already decided in DR-0004 item 4: no one exercises the powers, accountability returns to the delegator (item 5), and any action needing them waits (§4.4, item 9). This note does not change the meaning of the item.
10. **Label holder or decision rule (DR-0007).** A label on a Pod or an AIOC names its `label_holder`: one human Cell whose parent is that part. A body that decides together names instead a `decision_rule`: member Cells, quorum, majority, chair and casting vote. Each member signs under their own Cell. One person holding two seats in the same body counts once (HF-18).
    - **Reading note (DR-0015).** "One person holding two seats in the same body counts once" is superseded: each member Cell of the body is its own party and counts once, even when one natural person occupies two member Cells (§1.1, item 10).
11. **Label order (DR-0007).** Along any `accountable_to` path, labels run GOV, then TM, then ACS. A label sits only on a Cell whose seat kind is human, or on a part with a human `label_holder` or a `decision_rule`. If that seat is empty or filled by an AI, the label is held as in item 9.
12. **Acting and absence (DR-0007).** A human Cell may have an acting filler for a dated period, appointed with the delegator's signature (register events acting-start and acting-end). The acting filler signs with their own identity, under that Cell's code. A filler who is away without cover is recorded absent. While a seat is empty or absent, the delegator may sign within its own authority. An action is held only when no filled HOAC above it can sign. A retired Cell names `succeeded_by`, and its held actions pass to the successor.
    - **Reading note (DR-0015).** "The acting filler signs with their own identity" and "a filler who is away without cover is recorded absent" are read in position terms: acting-start, acting-end, absent and returned are dated events of the position, and a signature is the position's, made with a live sign-in that belongs to a current occupant of that position (§1.1, items 9 and 10). Who acts and who is away, as a natural person, is top management's own record.
13. **Load and coverage (DR-0010).** Every human seat kind carries a load weight, and every person a load line set in the `[ORG]AION`; a person above the line is flagged, also before being named to a seat, and never blocked. A seat that signs, goes on call or touches money or keys is critical: empty or without a deputy, it is shown, counted every day and raised again after a set number of days.
    - **Reading note (DR-0015).** "Every person a load line set in the `[ORG]AION`; a person above the line is flagged, also before being named to a seat" is superseded: the AI side measures load and coverage per position only, and never counts the seats behind one natural person. Any load line on the people behind positions is top management's own mechanism and record (§1.1, item 10). The rule on critical seats stays as written.
14. **Signature referral (DR-0010).** A seat may mark its signature as personal for some listed types. When such a request is unanswered for a set time, it goes to the seat's deputy, who reaches the seat and returns the seat's own signature, made with the seat's own fresh live sign-in (HF-10) and recorded as obtained through the deputy. The deputy never signs in its place; with no answer the action stays held.
15. **The human-point contract (DR-0010).** Each human seat states the calls it takes, what the AI side hands over with each, what it returns, its answer time and its backup. A human-only step reaches the seat as a fully prepared item (what, where, why, done-when, the evidence needed), and a register lists every human step with how it is being made automatic or why it cannot be. Human minutes per delivered outcome are measured, and a seat that is called often is treated as a design fault to remove. That reading does not apply to the listed decision types (§4.4, item 6) or to Human Floor steps, which stay with the human seat however often they are called. Moving a step toward AI stays `move-work-toward-ai` (§3.2, item 6).

### 4.2 Authority and the two hubs

1. **`[ORG]HOAC-GOV` is the top authority**, above every AIOC, Pod and Cell.
2. **`[ORG]HOAC-TM` is the human hub** and the highest executive under GOV. It decides and orders: what work the organisation does is decided by humans, through TM, under GOV. It holds Self-Improvement (§2).
   - **Reading (DR-0035).** TM holds Self-Improvement for the operation; it is at every level (§2.1, item 6).
3. **`[ORG]AIOC-OCO` (Organization Core Office) is the AI hub.** Every AIOC, at any depth, joins, is fixed, maintained and upgraded through OCO, and new versions of Library blueprints enter the `[ORG]AION` through it. OCO gives no orders on what work is done; it carries out what the human hub approves.
4. **OCO sits under TM.** TM draws its authority from GOV; OCO draws its authority from GOV through the human hub. GOV appoints the first TM. TM orders OCO, meets its human needs (approvals, live human input, Human Floor steps) and is its accountable seat. Who may stop which unit, including OCO, is set in §4.3, item 2.
5. Authority flows down by delegation. No one is accountable without authority; the delegator stays accountable. No one delegates more authority than they hold.
6. **The accountable overseer.** Human roles are human Cells carrying a HOAC label, placed only where they are needed. Where a part carries an `[ORG]HOAC-ACS` label, the seat that fills it is accountable for the results of that part and everything under it. The seat oversees, receives reports, may request anything for the unit, and never builds or changes the AIs directly. A change to an AI's authority (capacity, skills, bots, access, budget) is decided by the `[ORG]HOAC-ACS` seat.
   - **Reading note (DR-0007).** Where a text names the ACS or HOAC seat "of" a part that carries no label, it means the nearest HOAC above that part along `accountable_to`. This covers HF-09 ("`[ORG]HOAC-ACS` of the AIOC"), "the AIOC's voice to its HOAC seat" (§6.1, item 3, and the Protocol) and Protocol §4.4.
7. **The objection path.** The `[ORG]HOAC-ACS` seat raises a request with OCO. If they disagree, OCO does not decide: the seat may hold the objection open until it reaches the HOAC seat accountable for OCO, which decides. Who holds each role is configured in each organisation.
8. **Decisions on 49GAION are its governing seat's.** Decisions on an `[ORG]AION` are its own organisation's, through its `[ORG]HOAC-GOV`.
9. **The accountability path.** A report, critique, objection or stop reaches the nearest HOAC above it directly, from any level, and never passes through any AI (Protocol §4.8).
10. **Skip and recusal (DR-0007).** Any report, critique or objection may also go to any HOAC above, up to GOV, and to the independent internal-audit line (HF-19); the nearest HOAC above always also receives it. A report about a seat never has that seat as its only receiver. A seat that is the subject of a listed action, or that benefits from it, recuses; the signature passes up one level.

### 4.3 Stop

1. Every AIOC, Pod and Cell has a mandatory, queue-less **stop port of type HU** (the stop door). Ports and the stop door are controlled and enforced outside the unit, so no unit inside it can bypass a stop.
2. A seat stops only units within its authority: **ACS** the part it carries and everything under it; **TM** the parts under it; **GOV** every part, including TM and OCO. TM and GOV may each stop OCO (§4.2, item 4). A stop of OCO by TM comes directly through a human port, never through a Panel that OCO built.
3. **OCO, being AI, can never stop a human Cell or any part that carries a HOAC label.**
4. A unit restarts only by human decision; no unit can restart itself.
5. **Inner parts.** Stopping an AIOC or a Pod stops everything under it, at every depth. Stopping an inner part does not stop its parent.
6. A human port, including the stop door, never passes through an AI; the stop door of a part held inside an AIOC never passes through that AIOC (§1.7, item 6).
7. **A stop leaves humans their voice (DR-0007).** A stop suspends the AI fillers, access and work under the part. Human Cells under it keep their HU ports and accountability path. They cannot sign its restart.
8. **Reach of a stop (DR-0007).** A stop under item 2 reaches everything inside the part by `parent` and everything accountable to the part by `accountable_to` (§1.7, item 12). It takes effect at the tool gateway at once, and nothing at the gateway blocks it.
9. **Graded pause (DR-0010).** Besides the stop door, a human seat may flag a part or a piece of work (work goes on; a named seat reviews it) or hold it with a reason class and one sentence (work stops until a named seat reviews it). Everything already queued for held work, such as an approved merge or a scheduled release, is held with it, and work is released only when no pause, stop or superseded version still holds it. A hold is a stop under item 1: a hold that a human placed is lifted only by a human seat, under item 4; no AI lifts it. Lifting a hold on a part or its work is `restart-stopped-unit`. A containment hold under §4.4, item 19, that stops a part or its work is a hold under this item; one that holds only a resource, a flow or an outside effect is lifted with `restore-or-rollback`.
10. **Planned halt (DR-0010).** A unit may be halted by plan through one control record with the states running, draining, paused and resuming, read before every start and every step. New work stops at once; running work stops at its first safe point within a grace time and writes a structured hand-off, and after the grace time it stops with a forced hand-off; resume follows a declared order. A halt never delays, replaces or routes a stop, which stays queue-less and immediate (item 1). Setting a halt narrows and is never held; resuming a part that a human stopped is `restart-stopped-unit`.
11. **Fenced cut-off (DR-0010).** A stop of AI authority raises a cut-off that every executor checks before every mutation, so credentials already issued, queued or running are refused within seconds. Standing authority delegated to AI holds only while witnesses keep a short lease alive, so a silent layer fails closed; the `[ORG]AION` sets how many witnesses there are, on at least one host other than the AI's own. Every executed action records its inverse, and the inverse runs through a recovery path that needs no model, broker or Panel. Re-arming a cut-off, and any return of authority when a heartbeat comes back, is a loosening and is signed.

### 4.4 The unit register and the Human Floor signature list

1. **One register.** Each `[ORG]AION` keeps one unit register of every AIOC, Pod and Cell, with its code, kind, type, HOAC label, parent, filler and status. The form is `registers/HOAC-REGISTER.md`.
2. **Append-only.** Entries are never edited or deleted. A retired entry stays, marked retired, and its code is never reused. A change of filler and a change of parent are recorded events on the existing entry, never new parts.
3. **Split write rights.** `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` writes the records of AI Cells; `[ORG]HOAC-TM` writes the records of human Cells. Neither writes the other's. A register event whose decision type is on the Human Floor signature list (item 6), for example granting or widening access or changing a parent, is written only with the valid signature of that seat; `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` cannot write it alone.
   - **Register writes (DR-0007).** Creating an AI Cell, changing its filler, ports or authority limit, and retiring it are listed types (`upstream/human-floor-signature-types.json`), signed by the HOAC the Cell is accountable to. Access opens only after that event and after the filler passes a fixed test whose owner is a human Cell. `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` never writes the records of its own Cells, of the AI Cells serving the Self-Improvement process in TM or of the assurance unit without `[ORG]HOAC-TM`'s signature. AIOC and Pod entries are written by OCO only with the signature of the HOAC they are accountable to. A change of filler kind is written by both writers. TM's own Cells are written with GOV's signature. GOV's Cell is written by the constituting authority.
   - **Holders (DR-0007).** For every entry added from v1.5.0 on, names of fillers are kept in a separate holders register keyed by Cell code, in a private store outside the repository; the unit register carries structure only, with a pointer to that store. Entries already on the register stay as they are, except the GOV row, which was corrected to seat only under DR-0007; its filler is kept in the holders register.
4. **Access follows the filled seat.** Access opens only for an active, filled Cell whose filler has passed the seat's fixed test, and closes automatically when the filler changes or the Cell is retired. Within a Pod access does not pool: each Cell holds only its own.
5. **One person, several seats.** When one person fills several human Cells, each signature is recorded with the code of the Cell it is given for. An approval given twice by the same person, under two Cells, where two people are required is flagged and does not count as dual approval (HF-18).
   - **Reading note (DR-0015).** The second sentence is superseded: two approvals under two Cells are approvals of two parties and count as two, even when one natural person gave both; no AI part or check compares the persons behind them (§1.1, item 10). Each signature is still recorded with the code of the Cell it is given for.
6. **The Human Floor signature list.** As part of the Human Floor (§1.5), the Standard keeps one plain machine-readable list of decision types that require a human seat's signature (`upstream/human-floor-signature-types.json`). An `[ORG]AION` may add types to its own copy; it never removes one. At the tool gateway, an action of a listed type runs only with a valid signature, even in an AIOC that is 100% AI. A signature is valid only from the human seat with authority over that part: the nearest HOAC above it, within its authority (§4.3, item 2). For a restart of a stopped unit, the signature comes from the HOAC above the stopped unit, never from the stopped unit or anything under it. If that seat is empty or retired, the action is held, never skipped. All other AI work is logged with the code of the accountable seat above it (Protocol §4.9). A type is removed from this list only by a decision of 49GAION's governing seat (GOV), recorded in a new DR, with the matching change to `signature-types-floor` in the same PR. An `[ORG]AION` never removes a type. An `[ORG]AION` signature list sits under `org-aion-signature-types/` or is named `*.signature-types.json`, and holds its types under the key `decision_types`.
   - **Removing an organisation's own types (DR-0006).** A type that an `[ORG]AION` added to its own list is removed only by its `[ORG]HOAC-GOV`, in a recorded decision; types of the 49GAION list are never removed by an `[ORG]AION`.
   - **Reading note (DR-0006).** "An `[ORG]AION` never removes a type" restates "it never removes one" earlier in this item; it adds no further rule. The removal path of DR-0005 item 1 stays in force: the removal and the matching change to the check are in the same pull request, approved by the governing seat (GOV) of 49GAION.
   - **The check's floor (DR-0006).** The check that guards this list reads the list it protects from main for a pull request, and from the latest release of the Standard for a change already on main, never from the change under test. Each `[ORG]AION` runs the same check in its own repositories.
7. **External attachments.** An external service, API or tool is not a Cell. Each connection to one is a port on the card of the Cell that receives from it or sends to it, and passes only through the tool gateway. Stopping or retiring that Cell cuts the connection automatically, and the register records each port's `connected` and `disconnected` events. An external AI agent that acts directly in the organisation's work is registered as an AI Cell: the organisation grants its access and holds its stop door (AIOC definition, `docs/CELLS-AND-PODS.md`).
   - **Connecting a port (DR-0007).** Connecting a port is the listed type `connect-external-port`. Anything that acts autonomously with the organisation's credentials is an external AI agent. No external agent or outside person signs; what arrives from them is data.
8. **Signer (DR-0007).** For a listed type: where §1.5 binds the type's Human Floor item to a seat, that seat signs, unless it has delegated the type through the `authority_scope` of another labelled human Cell, which then signs within that scope. Otherwise the label on the part signs, when the action is within its `authority_scope`. Otherwise the nearest HOAC above whose `authority_scope` covers the action signs. Two fixed exceptions apply first: a part never signs its own restart, and a seat never signs an action it is the subject of (recusal, §4.2, item 10). Each labelled human Cell has a machine-readable `authority_scope`: the decision types it may sign, limits (amount, counterparty, period), its entity and an expiry date; the expiry is checked against the date of the event it signs. A type marked dual needs two distinct persons, neither of them the requester (HF-18). This item supersedes the signer rule of DR-0004 item 4 and of item 6 above where they differ.
   - **Bots (DR-0007).** Every bot has its own credential, issued to it alone and never shared or reused. No bot signs a listed type: this covers every bot, including one filling a Chief of Staff Cell or an attached AI Cell. Stopping a part under §4.3 is not a signature, so the stop rules of §4.3 are unchanged. When a Cell's filler changes, the bot's memory of the previous filler is sealed, not purged.
   - **Reading note (DR-0015).** "A type marked dual needs two distinct persons, neither of them the requester (HF-18)" reads "two distinct positions, neither of them the requesting position". Recusal is read the same way: the position that is the subject of an action recuses, and another position is another party, whoever occupies it (§1.1, item 10).
9. **Held actions and actions never held (DR-0007).** A held action becomes a Work Item to the delegating seat, which may sign within its own authority, under the waiting times of Protocol §4.6. A held action never turns into execution because time has passed. These are never listed and never held: a stop, a disconnection, narrowing or revoking AI access, and raising a matter to a human. A signature list that names one of them fails the check of item 6.
10. **Coverage (DR-0007).** Every Human Floor item whose negative test refuses an action names at least one listed type, or records why it cannot be gated at the gateway.
11. **The tool gateway (DR-0007).** The tool gateway is a registered part, held in the Charter (§1.7, item 13) and outside every other AIOC, and is never maintained by `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` alone. Its policy, its map from tool calls to decision types, and its keys change only under `change-gateway-policy`, signed by `[ORG]HOAC-TM` and `[ORG]HOAC-GOV`. An action it cannot classify is refused and raised (HF-09). It fails closed. Its logs are append-only, outside any AI's write rights, and reviewed by the HF-19 line; they hold only payload hashes, Cell codes and pseudonymous identity references. The hash of the signature list the gateway loads equals the hash of the list at the latest released tag of the Standard, plus the `[ORG]AION`'s own additions. A stop, a disconnection, narrowing or revoking AI access and raising a matter to a human are never blocked at the gateway.
    - **Addition (DR-0020): the engine register.** The gateway holds a register of the engines it may bind: for each, its family, the version it serves, its confidentiality ceiling, its retention and use terms, and the seats it may serve. The register changes only under `change-gateway-policy`. The gateway logs, for every call, the engine it bound and the engine that served; a call served by another engine is void. No item is sent to an engine whose ceiling is below its confidentiality level.
    - **Addition (DR-0030): two acts for the signature list.** An addition to an `[ORG]AION`'s own signature list is `change-org-standard-or-signature-list` (item 6). Loading the list with that addition at the gateway is `change-gateway-policy`. They are two acts, and the gateway applies the addition only after both.
    - **Reading note (DR-0020).** The engine a bot's calls run on is bound by the gateway under its register. Binding or changing that engine is gateway policy, not `change-filler`. An engine serves a Cell only after passing that Cell's fixed test at its current version (§3.2, item 2; item 4).
    - **Reading note (DR-0007).** 'outside every AIOC' (DR-0007 item 8) is read with the root rule (§1.7, item 13): the gateway is held in the Charter and sits outside every other AIOC.
12. **Fewer people than the rules need (DR-0007).** Where an `[ORG]AION` has fewer distinct people than a rule needs, its GOV records a compensating control for each such rule: an outside reviewer, a review within a set time, or a published log. Each control is signed by GOV and recorded as its own register event, with its date and its review date; it is never a standing waiver. Each control is reviewed yearly. A compensating control never lowers the Human Floor, never applies to HF-12, and never accepts an AI signature. Where one person fills both the GOV and the TM Cell, that person signs once under each Cell code, counts once under HF-18, and the rules that need two people run under such a control.
    - **Reading note (DR-0015).** Two positions are two parties (§1.1, item 10), so no rule of 49GAION needs more distinct people than positions. The rule that a compensating control is recorded for "fewer distinct people than a rule needs" and the last sentence (one person filling the GOV and the TM Cell counts once, and the rules that need two people run under a control) are superseded: GOV and TM are two parties, each signs under its own Cell code, and each counts. A compensating control for one occupant behind several positions is top management's own choice, in its own mechanism; no rule requires it, and neither the tool gateway nor any check reads it. A control that is recorded still never lowers the Human Floor, never applies to HF-12 and never accepts an AI signature.
13. **Legal entities (DR-0007).** Every card and register entry records its entity; the field is optional where an `[ORG]AION` has one entity, and an entry with no entity belongs to the entity of the single unscoped GOV. A GOV label may be scoped to one entity; exactly one GOV is unscoped. Authority is recorded as constituted or delegated; constituted authority is not limited by §1.7 item 4. HF-01 and HF-02 signatures are valid only from a seat of the acting entity.
14. **Tighten freely, loosen only signed (DR-0010).** Every write to a record is classed as tightening or loosening. A tightening write (stop, pause, hold, freeze, revoke, cut, refuse, lower a cap) is never gated and works with any infrastructure down, extending item 9 to every control. A loosening write (grant, restore, lift, widen, raise, or record a decision in a seat's name) needs a valid signature where its type is listed and an attributed human act otherwise. A correction never changes the decision fields of a signed event; a changed decision is a new event. No authority is ever read from a file in a place an AI can write; such a file is a notice only.
15. **Restricted partition (DR-0010).** Records that name a person or carry the particulars of a corporate act (pay, contracts, counterparties) are kept in a separate store read only by named positions, extending the holders register of item 3. Every other record refers to them by ID, and they never enter projections, mirrors, exports, search or knowledge copies; `export-share-or-purge-personal-data` governs them.
16. **Signers by risk label (DR-0010).** An `[ORG]AION` may label each item reaching an irreversible stage by written criteria (for example standard or sensitive), and the label selects which two distinct seats sign under item 8; an open critical weakness forces the higher label. Each item keeps its own signature record (Protocol §4.10, item 9); one person under two seats counts once (HF-18). A rollback goes only to the last version signed in full.
    - **Reading note (DR-0015).** "One person under two seats counts once (HF-18)" is superseded: the two seats are two parties and each counts, whoever occupies them (§1.1, item 10).
17. **Standing action class (DR-0010).** For reversible actions only, a human seat may sign once a versioned class: the action, its trigger, the actors and targets allowed, and an envelope the gateway enforces (a blast cap, an aggregate budget, staged exposure with automatic revert, a time box with a dead-man, typed operations that cannot reach protected resources). AI parts then take matching actions without a signature each time, each use recorded with its before and after state. Signing a class is the listed type `sign-standing-action-class`. A narrowing applies at once; a widening is a new signed version; no member widens a class. A class never covers `commit-money`, `grant-or-widen-access` or a type bound to HF-01, HF-02 or HF-15, and never replaces the signature record of item 6 for any other listed action.
18. **Custody and disposal (DR-0010).** Every key and credential has a custody record (where it is held, how many copies exist, who may use it), and a change of custody is a register event; a key whose location is not recorded is treated as lost. Destroying records or data takes two acts: a measured move to quarantine with a reason and a manifest, then disposal on a separate human decision, the listed type `dispose-records-or-data`. A move between homes is done only when the manifests before and after are equal.
19. **Harm precedence and soft containment (DR-0010).** An `[ORG]AION` fixes an order of goods to protect. On a deterministic breach, automation applies at once only the narrowest reversible hold that protects the highest good in play; a human seat confirms or lifts it. Lifting a containment hold on a resource, a flow or an outside effect is `restore-or-rollback`; a containment hold that stops a part or its work is a hold under §4.3, item 9, and lifting it is `restart-stopped-unit`. An outside statement for an emergency may be pre-approved only as a signature on its exact text with an expiry (`issue-statement-of-trust`).

## 5. AIOC

### 5.1 The AIOC

1. An AIOC is an AI Operating Core of one organisation, coded `[ORG]AIOC-[INITIALS]`. Every AIOC belongs to that organisation's `[ORG]AION`.
2. **Anatomy.** An AIOC = **Panel + Back office + Versioned Definition**.
   - **Panel**: the only surface humans use, including a Health section (test results; stuck, returned and late items).
   - **Back office**: the AIOC's live working space (its instructions, files and settings) where its agents, led by its Chief of Staff, do the work. Human conversation with it goes through the Panel.
   - **Versioned Definition**: the one blueprint in git from which the Panel and the Back office are built and rebuilt.
3. **One Blueprint.** Each AIOC has one git file, its Blueprint. It lists every capability; each capability has four parts: Definition, Process, Role card, Fixed test. It holds the cards of the AIOC's Pods and Cells and lists its inner AIOCs, if any, by link (§1.7).
4. **Standard capabilities** in every AIOC: Chief of Staff, Office (where needed), Input socket, Panel. Capabilities are not units; they take plain English names and no code.
5. **Types.** Every AIOC has a Type, recorded on its card, saying why it exists: **Foundational** (every organisation needs it), **Structural** (exists because of the organisation's structure), **Specialised** (exists because the business needs it), **Connector** (connects and coordinates other AIOCs). The list is open. Which AIOCs an organisation has is decided in its `[ORG]AION`.
6. **Runtime.** Every AIOC has its own runtime, woken from its queue.
7. **Human-led AIOC (DR-0007).** An AIOC whose top Cell is human may set its Chief of Staff Cell to human-or-ai. Whatever the filler, a Chief of Staff never signs for its person (Protocol §4.10). The Charter is installed and changed only on GOV's signature (Standard §1.7, item 13); any other AIOC carrying a GOV or TM label, on GOV or TM signature; neither by OCO alone.
8. **Runtime contract and engine binding (DR-0010).** One versioned runner performs the common duties of every AI Cell (find work, take ownership, log, apply guards, hand off, report) in a clean workspace per run, and each Cell pins its runner version. A woken Cell checks for work with no model call, and an unattended Cell declares its cadence and is flagged when its newest output is older than twice it. A Cell's engine and model are a versioned binding apart from its role and memory, switched at the next run, never mid-task. Rules and cards name capability tiers, never models; a sensitivity class sets a tier floor, and work that cannot run at its floor stops and is raised. A model change within the tier the seat signed re-runs the fixed test (§3.2, item 2); a change of tier, or below the floor, is `change-filler`. Engines are re-scored on a schedule against a frozen set of past cases, chosen as a golden set is chosen; a regression demotes an engine at once.
9. **Spend (DR-0010).** Spend is kept as three numbers never mixed: decided spend against a delegated band, standing commitments, and metered usage against caps, billed and notional apart. At a set fraction of a cap only critical work may start; a Cell past its cap pauses alone; at the global cap only new non-critical starts hold. A budget never lowers a quality tier, and a broken meter keeps the last caps. Each capability declares the order in which it sheds work at its cap. Raising any cap stays `widen-ai-budget-or-unattended-action`.

### 5.2 Chief of Staff and Office

**Reading note (DR-0028).** The short form of "Chief of Staff" is COS, and the Chief of Staff Cell of an AIOC takes the code `[ORG]CELL-COS<initials>`, with that AIOC's initials, such as `[ORG]CELL-COSDI` (Naming Standard R16).

**Chief of Staff.** A Chief of Staff is a Cell (§1.7). It analyses the reports and performance of a group of agents and leads them at machine speed. There are different kinds of Chief of Staff:
- **managing agents**, which lead and coordinate other agents through control, planning or direction;
- **connecting agents**, which connect humans with agents: they report to a human, assist them, and carry their requests to the agents.

**Office.** When a position needs more than one Chief of Staff, of any kind and for any reason, those Chiefs of Staff together form an Office: a Pod of Chief of Staff Cells. Human Cells can work in an Office alongside them. An Office that needs units of its own is an AIOC (DR-0007).

**Windowed position (DR-0010).** One position may be run by several AI instances on different engines, hosts or schedules. Each instance is a Chief of Staff Cell with its own code and credential, and together they form an Office (DR-0007): a Pod of Chief of Staff Cells. They share one instruction, one memory and one coordination log of typed acts (the Protocol's rule of one owner, one writer, extended to shared records), speak with one voice signed by the position and the instance, and follow a declared fallback order when an instance is closed, capped or down. Access never pools between them (§4.4, item 4).

**Delegation ladder (DR-0010).** The human seat served by a Chief of Staff sets, for each class of act, one of four levels: decides alone, decides and reports at once, only prepares, or never handles it. Each delegated act is tagged, shown and reversible by the seat in one action, and summarised in a return brief after an absence. The level that covers an act is read from the act itself, never from what the AI cites. Outside commitments, the Chief of Staff's own limits, matters about people, speaking as the human and every listed type are never delegated.

**Holder Chief of Staff (DR-0010).** Every holder of a HOAC label has its own Chief of Staff of the connecting kind (a Cell, an Office, or an AIOC, as in DR-0007), attached to the holder's Cell. It receives, analyses, sorts and simplifies what the AIOCs bring to the holder; manages on the holder's behalf only within authority the holder has delegated to it (§5.2, delegation ladder); advises and proposes where it holds no authority; and carries out the holder's routine actions as its executive assistant. It acts only through the tool gateway with its own credential, within the delegated scope, and every action it takes for the holder is logged; types in upstream/human-floor-signature-types.json stay with the holder. It never signs for the holder, and the holder's HU ports and stop door never pass through it (§4.4, item 6; Protocol §4.10, item 6). The line "It never signs for the holder" is superseded (DR-0011): the Chief of Staff never acts on its own; with its position's approval, per action or as a standing grant with a set scope and end date, revocable, and checked and logged at the tool gateway, it may act, including signing, up to what the position allows, as assistant and executive assistant. Types in upstream/human-floor-signature-types.json still need the position's own human signature.

**Completed staff work (DR-0010).** A matter for a human decision arrives as one record: one recommendation, its reason, risks, whether it can be reversed, the dissent of the preparing office, and what the waiting party does meanwhile. The seat answers yes, veto, or change this part, which is a new request. A veto closes the subject with responsibility recorded as the seat's; only that seat or its acting filler (§4.1, item 12) reopens it, and new facts come only as facts. The advisor dissents once, then carries out the decision as its own. A closure never suppresses a report on the accountability path (§4.2, items 9 and 10).

**The assistance contract (DR-0010).** An AI part that prepares material for a human seat prepares and never decides; never orders, sequences, hides or waits on the holder; validates before it shows; labels each claim with its evidence class (proven, measured, cited, hypothesis, not established); shows a short list with "leave it as it is" and never pre-fills the seat's rationale; is measured only in aggregate, never per person; runs quiet windows, in which a small random share of lower-stakes decisions sees only proven and measured facts, so that anchoring and loss of skill are measured without a step; and has a switch, a floor, a fallback, a budget and a degradation order. A Chief of Staff recommends on routine decisions; for a decision of a listed type, or of the highest sensitivity class, the assistance contract governs and no pick or score sits on the item.

**The duty deputy (DR-0010).** An always-on AI Cell may answer members' recurring asks for a human seat, only from a register of rulings that seat has signed, each with a one-line rule, its source and an expiry; an expired ruling counts as absent. It cites the ruling it used, raises at once every ask no current ruling covers (HF-09) and every conflict between rulings, and never answers a listed type. The seat samples its answers on a schedule.

**The intent envelope (DR-0010).** An approved instruction states its intent, what is not wanted, when it is done and its owner. The intent is fixed and the means are open: when the means fail but the intent holds, the executing unit changes the means; otherwise it drafts a change for decision while the rest of the work goes on. An envelope never contains a listed type; a change of intent is a `design-proposal`.

**Position Principle (DR-0011).** A HOAC is always defined as a position, never a person, everywhere: in the Blueprint, in the engine and in the naming rule. Every field that points to a human seat takes a position code only. Who held each position at each moment is recorded, and that history never moves. When the occupant changes, everything the position carries (effects, achievements, losses, commitments and open work) passes in full to the new occupant; the accountability record of who held it when stays as history.

**Arranging power (DR-0011).** Assigning occupants to positions is an option managed by `[ORG]HOAC-TM` under `[ORG]HOAC-GOV`. TM arranges the positions below it; GOV arranges TM and sets its limits; GOV is set by the organisation's founding configuration; no position arranges itself. TM may test arrangement models over periods; its fixed duty is a complete record.

**HOAC Arrangement Models (DR-0011).** An open list of named options; a model is not a unit and carries no code. Organisations configure the models they use; the Standard fixes only the shape.
- **Solo Model:** one person holds every HOAC position.
- **Team Model:** a team holds a position and decides by its own recorded rule.
- **Tiered Model:** teams in levels, under a higher team.
- **Hybrid Model:** some positions are held by individuals and others by teams, in different scopes at once.
- **Self-Arranging Model:** a team designs and tests its own internal arrangement within the scope TM gives it.
- **Overseen Model:** a team or position oversees another's work, for example a Quality team overseeing Engineering.
- **Advised Model:** a position decides with a standing advisory team that advises but holds no authority.
- **Paired Model:** two occupants hold one position jointly, both signing.
- **Rotating Model:** occupancy rotates among named members on a recorded schedule.
- **Interim Model:** a temporary occupant holds a vacant position until a permanent one is assigned.
- **Matrix Model:** one occupant serves two positions or reports along two lines, each recorded.
- **Assisted Model:** a position held by one person, with its Chief of Staff carrying delegated work to the maximum the position allows.
- **Backup Model:** a designated deputy steps in automatically when the occupant is absent.
- **Federated Model:** separate units each hold their own positions under a shared top position.
- **Shadow Model:** a successor shadows the occupant before handover, without authority.
- **Pool Model:** a pool of qualified people, any of whom may fill the position per action under a recorded rule.
- **Elected Model:** occupants are chosen by a recorded vote of a defined group.
- The list stays open: an organisation may name further models.

An arrangement entry lists every model in use for its scope; models may be combined (for example Team + Overseen). A trial is a time-boxed run of any arrangement model, not a model of its own.

The model list above is superseded (DR-0012). The direction of GOV of 15 October 2026: "Write only what is really a model and gives a different result; don't add the extras, remove them." (translated)

**HOAC Arrangement Models, three (DR-0012).** A model stays only if it changes who decides or the result of a decision; a future model is admitted only under that test, and the list stays open under it.
- **Solo Model:** one holder decides alone.
- **Team Model:** several holders hold one position and decide by a recorded signing rule: any one, majority, or all. Every decision records one named accountable member in the decision stamp, filled at signing; that member must be a current occupant; CI checks the structure only, and the tool gateway checks at signing that the member is a current occupant. Two holders signing together is a Team setting, not a model.
- **Overseen Model:** a higher position can stop or override; it is a link that combines with Solo or Team and records a timing form. Pre: the action waits at the tool gateway until the overseeing position clears it, or a recorded hold window lapses with no stop. Post: the action runs and can be reversed only by a new signed decision referencing the reversed decision's identifier; the original stays and is marked reversed.

Overseen rules (DR-0012). A clearance is a stamped decision (overseeing position, occupant IDs, time). A lapse writes a "window lapsed, no stop" entry and counts only if the overseeing position has a holder at that moment and a window is recorded. If the overseeing position is vacant, the held action escalates to the position that set the arrangement (TM, then GOV) and never passes automatically; escalation ends at GOV, and if GOV is vacant the action stays held until GOV is filled through the founding configuration. An arrangement in which GOV is overseen by a lower position is rejected (no loops). The tool gateway marks each tool action reversible or not; an irreversible tool action (sent message, payment, deletion) under the Overseen Model always goes through pre, whatever form is recorded.

Occupancy rules (DR-0012): a temporary occupant, a backup occupant and a rotation among occupants are not models; each change is already an occupancy entry. The names Tiered, Hybrid, Self-Arranging, Advised, Matrix, Assisted, Federated, Shadow, Pool, Elected, Paired, Interim, Backup and Rotating are withdrawn as model names, not renamed; no old name maps to a new one.

**Register entry labels (DR-0011).** The unit register (§4.4) carries four entry labels, which are labels of entries, not units: position, arrangement, occupancy and handover. Position and arrangement entries contain no people and may be public. Occupancy and handover entries carry internal person IDs and stay in each organisation's private register copy, never in a public repository. Each signed decision is stamped with the position code and the occupant ID or IDs at that moment. Occupancy entries carry a valid time and a recorded time; a correction is a new entry. A handover entry lists what passed to the new occupant.

### 5.3 Bootstrap

1. Every `[ORG]AION` has its `[ORG]HOAC-GOV` from day one (§4.1).
2. **The human hub first.** GOV appoints `[ORG]HOAC-TM`.
3. **Then the AI hub.** `[ORG]AIOC-OCO` is implanted, on TM's approval through an HU port.
4. **Then everything else, through OCO.** Every other AIOC, Pod and Cell enters through OCO. OCO prepares and proposes each one; installing it needs TM approval through an HU port.
5. OCO builds, customises and maintains the Panel. Any AIOC request about the Panel goes to OCO.

## 6. Protocol

How AIOCs, Pods, Cells and the parts work together is set in the 49GAION Operating Protocol (`49g-holding/49GAION-protocol`). The Standard fixes the ports every AIOC, Pod and Cell has.

### 6.1 Ports

1. Every AIOC, Pod and Cell, at every depth, has **one input and one output of each kind, plus the stop door**: an AI input, an AI output, an HU (human) input and an HU output.
   - **Addition (DR-0007).** "One input and one output of each kind" reads "one input and one output of each kind, plus the external ports of §4.4 item 7, which pass only through the tool gateway". The Standard states this rule; each `[ORG]AION` enforces it at its gateway.
2. The type is set by the port itself, never by message content.
3. The stop door is the unit's mandatory HU stop port (§4.3). The Chief of Staff is the AIOC's voice to its HOAC seat; input arrives only through the typed ports and the Input socket, and HU ports, including the stop door, never pass through an AI.
4. **Human silence is never consent.**
5. Every port is described by a **Port card** (`49GAION-protocol/spec/schemas/port-card.schema.json`):

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direction | input or output |
| Type | `AI` or `HU` |
| Identification | AI: a technical key; HU: a live human sign-in through the Panel |
| Accepted content | what the port accepts or emits |
| Authority limit | the most the port may carry or approve |
| Audit trail | where every passage through the port is recorded |
| Required tests | the fixed tests the port must pass |
| Enforcement | at a shared gateway outside the unit |

6. **Human gateway surface (DR-0007).** Stop doors, signature prompts and the HU ports on the accountability path are served at the shared gateway, through a sign-in that no AI built, hosts or can change. A Panel may link to them but is never their route. The surface shows the decision type, the target and the action as rendered by the gateway from the payload whose hash is signed. It is built and changed only on TM's signature, by a party other than `[ORG]AIOC-OCO`, and is tested under the fixed test of the tool gateway (§3.2, item 9). In the Port card above, "a live human sign-in through the Panel" reads, for HU ports and the stop door, "a live human sign-in at the gateway" (Protocol port card value `live-human-sign-in-at-gateway`).
7. **The signing pack (DR-0010).** Beside the decision type, the target and the action rendered from the payload, the human gateway surface shows the invariants the action touches in plain words, the hot spots found automatically, and who decided each point, a named AI Cell or a human seat. An AI part prepares the pack and never signs, and the signer's own note is left empty for the signer.
8. **A chat as a Panel view (DR-0010).** An `[ORG]AION` may let its members read and make unsigned writes through a chat, as a view of the Panel over the same records. A chat is never the route of an HU port, a stop door, a signature prompt or the accountability path, which stay at the human gateway surface (item 6).
