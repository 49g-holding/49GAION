# Reviews of the first `[ORG]AION` capability, draft v0.1 (raw) · Structural Adaptation (DR-0040)

Asked on 25 October 2026, about 01:35 UTC, through a reviewer server. The draft was written by one engine (Claude). Two other engines challenged it, each given the same draft and the same three questions: what is wrong, vague or missing; a better name; the three changes to make first. At most 250 words each. The answers are kept as received.

## OpenAI (gpt-6-astra)

1. What is wrong, vague or missing. Authority slips: "keeps in balance" and "imposes" imply control; the core proposes organisational changes; it neither runs operations nor decides staffing or spending. Balance is undefined: lines need not grow together; spare capacity, compliance readiness and investment ahead of demand may be necessary; "only as work grows" could make the organisation permanently late. Split triggers are too loose: a distinct standard or rhythm does not necessarily justify another unit; consider extra capacity, clearer interfaces or process redesign first. Merge triggers are unsafe: idle capacity may provide resilience; apparent overlap may preserve independent assurance or separation of duties. Costs and failure modes are absent: splits add coordination, interfaces, seats, migration costs and potential duplication; repeated splitting and merging can destabilise delivery. Governance is incomplete: which human seat decides across unit boundaries? Two challenging engines do not guarantee independent scrutiny; a restore point cannot undo every staffing, contractual or customer consequence. Terms hide requirements: "Human Floor", "card", "ports", "fixed test" and "stop door" need explicit definitions or references.
2. Better name: Structural Adaptation; it covers splitting, merging, resizing and deliberate non-change, without promising an undefined balance.
3. First three changes: define decision rules (measurable capacity, quality, risk and human-load limits; observation periods; exceptions; safeguards against repeated reversals); require an options case (no change, process repair, added capacity, split and merge, with transition costs and protected independence); specify accountable change control (decision owner, staged transition, operational safeguards, success criteria, review date and realistic rollback limits).

## Gemini (gemini-4-argon)

1. What is wrong, vague or missing. "Balance" is unmeasured: without objective cross-unit metrics (for example sales intake exceeding operations capacity by X%), the core cannot generate valid proposals. Subjective triggers: "needs its own rhythm" and "sits idle" are not computable; the metrics in item 2 are not tied to the actions in items 3 and 4. Transition cost and thrashing: without cooldown periods or hysteresis, fluctuating workloads will make the core endlessly propose budding and merging the same unit. Scope creep in item 5: proposing to slow a line of work (for example throttling sales) or reallocate resources is operational management, not structural design, and violates the rule that the core never runs operations. Undefined jargon: Human Floor, card, ports, fixed test, stop door, three engines.
2. Better name: Structural Scaling (or Cellular Scaling); "Balanced Growth" sounds like a financial strategy, and "budding" ignores merging; the capability is about right-sizing structure to match operational load.
3. First three changes: link metrics to triggers with thresholds; add a cost-of-change threshold (a split or merge must gain more than its disruption costs); restrict item 5 to structural proposals, not operational throttling.

## What was applied, and what was not

Applied in the text of DR-0040 (Standard §1.3, item 1): the core proposes and never controls; demand signed or forecast is read as well as today's load; thresholds set by the organisation, held for a set period, with time to settle; an options case with gains and costs; merging never joins a part with its checker or removes required spare capacity or separation of duties; the balance is shown, not run; the human seat holding the affected units decides, with a staged transition, success measures, a review date and a restore point, stating what a restore cannot undo.

The name: taken from the challengers, on the governing seat's direction. "Structural Adaptation" (OpenAI) was chosen over "Structural Scaling" (Gemini).

Not applied: defining the Human Floor, card, ports, fixed test and stop door in the capability's text. They are defined once in 49GAION, and the text points to them.
