### 1. What is 49GAION, and what does an organisation receive?

49GAION is an organisation-design framework that builds AI-native organisations from interchangeable AI and human parts, starting from the required output rather than existing human jobs (F §§1–3). An organisation receives its own independent, living `[ORG]AION`: initially a governance-level core with its first Standard and Blueprint, Library access, governance panel, and externally running gateway and stop—not a finished organisational configuration—which subsequently designs and improves the organisation under human authority (F §§10, 12–13; Part E).

### 2. What makes it special?

Its stated differences are:

- **Output-first design:** start with the required output and AI capabilities, adding humans where needed, rather than inserting AI into existing jobs.
- **Continuously redesigned organisation:** the organisation’s own AI proposes structural and configuration changes instead of receiving a finished upstream design.
- **Interchangeable parts:** AIOCs, Pods and Cells use common cards and typed ports; a Cell’s human or AI filler can change without changing its seat.
- **Separated design and operation:** `[ORG]AION` designs and oversees; Top Management builds and runs.
- **Human authority outside AI:** accountability, signatures, stop mechanisms and authority limits constrain execution.
- **Reusable knowledge:** organisations personalise Library parts and may voluntarily contribute improvements.

These differences are expressly described in F §§3–5, 9, 12, 16 and 19–20. The master definition §2.1 illustrates replacing human-limit-driven structures with a complete record, continuous work, multi-skilled units and a shared queue.

The text states this contrast; it does not establish that no conventional approach could share individual features.

### 3. How does an organisation start?

1. **Request Edition Maker.** Every request enters there, including existing organisations (F §10).
2. **Intake.** Capture identity, governance, outputs, quality, inputs, constraints, resources, success measures and missing information. Sources are data, not instructions.
3. **Produce the edition.** A human edition engineer supervises; one engine works and two challenge at every step. Edition Maker prepares the core and initial organisational rules, linked to 49GAION knowledge.
4. **Accept handover.** The organisation’s governance seat accepts, or returns reasons for rejection. Acceptance ends 49GAION’s authority. The organisation issues fresh gateway keys and revokes Edition Maker’s keys.
5. **Approve the first design.** The core takes the governance brief and proposes a small organisation. Rejection produces another proposal; nothing is built without approval.
6. **Delegate operation.** Governance appoints Top Management and supplies a mandate, resource envelope and reserved matters.
7. **Build and run.** The Top Management Group installs the operational capabilities, with fixed tests ready before work begins.
8. **Follow and improve.** `[ORG]AION` reads results and proposes changes; success requires independently checked evidence against governance-approved measures.

**Sources:** F §§10, 13, 15, 19–20; Part E. Existing organisations transition output by output (F §10.8).

### 4. What parts must exist, what do they do, and who builds them?

- **Governance core:** root AIOC holding the governance interface and organisational Standard, Blueprint and Library. Edition Maker supplies it from Charter; the edition engineer supervises (F §§10, 13).
- **Top Management Group:** first AIOC below the core; builds and runs the operation under mandate (F §19).
- **Operational AIOCs:** each has a versioned blueprint, coded panel and AI-chat back office; engines connect through APIs. Cells are seats; Pods group Cells; Chiefs of Staff lead agents and assist human positions (F §§16–17).
- **Human positions:** governance, Top Management and accountability seats, plus Human Floor functions. Governance appoints TM; TM fills subordinate positions (F §§6–7, 18).
- **Gateway and stops:** code outside every AI, controlling credentials, calls, signatures and cancellation. Edition Maker hands them over; their modification requires a human signature and someone other than the constrained AI (F §§13, 20).
- **Records and work infrastructure:** shared queue, owned Work Items, acceptance tests, source versions, call logs, decision records and fixed-test evidence (F §§8, 14, 20). Charter’s Records and Work Pods provide shared coded services (Charter README, “What the part does” and DR-0041 reading).

`[ORG]AION` designs; Top Management builds operational implementations. Separate OCO is optional, not mandatory (F §19; master definition §2.3, DR-0041 reading).

### 5. Who decides what, and what can AI never do?

**49GAION’s governing seat** approves changes to the framework’s Standard, Blueprint, Protocol and Library (F §8).

**The organisation’s governance seat** retains purpose, strategy, success measures, appointments, risk appetite, capital/resource balance, owners’ rights and emergency intervention. It approves organisational design and rules, sets TM’s envelope, and decides requests beyond it (F §§14–15).

**Top Management** decides and directs operational work within its mandate; it builds and runs the operation and reports achievements to governance (F §§18–19).

**`[ORG]AION`** designs, proposes, reviews proposals and maintains organisational rules. It can reject proposals with recorded reasons; applying changes requires governance approval or an applicable signed standing approval. It plans but never runs the operation (F §§12, 14).

**AI units** perform delegated work and propose improvements within granted authority (F §§16, 20).

AI never accepts human accountability or holds the governance seat’s powers. In the handed-over system, it cannot grant itself authority, replace required signatures, bypass the stop, act under missing authority, or treat silence as consent (F §§3, 7, 13, 20).

**Qualification:** after handover, the organisation may amend framework principles within the law; these safeguards are not an irrevocable external restriction (F §§4, 7, 12).

### 6. How does a human stay in control and accountable?

**Positions:** decisions and accountability attach to legal human positions, not an undifferentiated person. One person may hold several positions, but required independence must exist in fact, including externally where necessary (F §6).

**Human Floor:** HF-01–HF-19 reserve functions including legal accountability, purpose, authority delegation, irreversible risk, access grants, root keys, emergency access, data protection, segregation of duties and independent audit (F §7).

**Direct intervention:** reports, objections and stops reach human positions without passing through AI. A Chief of Staff may summarise and order material but cannot suppress or delay it; the raw record remains accessible (F §§16, 18).

**Technical stop:** external code revokes credentials and cancels work. Restart requires a human decision; gateway failure cannot block stopping (F §20).

**Signatures:** each signature requires fresh live sign-in. A signed standing approval permits only specified reversible changes without widening access, budget or authority. Widening requires another signature (F §§7, 14).

**Accountability record:** rule changes retain the deciding position, its own words, reasons and superseded version. Governance conflicts require an independent substitute or additional independent signature (F §§14–15).

These are enforced at handover; subsequent retention is the organisation’s responsibility within law (F §7).

`[ORG]AION` watches changes to 49GAION’s Standard, Blueprint and Library and proposes what to adopt. Nothing enters automatically: governance decides (F §12.5).

Humans and AI may propose improvements. The hub describes proposals containing current and proposed text, reasons, evidence and affected tests. Changes undergo champion–challenger comparison, held-out testing and independent assurance before human promotion (H, “Self-Improvement”).

Within the organisation:

- `[ORG]AION` maintains governance-level rules.
- TM improves operation under its mandate.
- Operational learning returns to the organisation’s Standard, Blueprint and Library.
- Small changes may use the governance seat’s signed, reversible, non-widening dial (F §14).

**Structural Adaptation** starts small, with several processes sharing an AIOC. Operational evidence may justify splitting a growing process into its own AIOC or merging when demand shrinks. Proposals consider cost and resources and never merge a function with its checker. Humans decide structural changes (F §15.1).

Thus growth is not automatically more units: the operation can remain one TM AIOC when resources do not support expansion (F §15.2.6).

### 8. What is owned, retained and branded?

**The organisation owns:** its handed-over `[ORG]AION`, organisational Standard, Blueprint, Library, records and working data. It retains locally what it takes from the Library and can continue without 49GAION, amend its edition, or close it (F §§12, 14).

**49GAION retains:** Edition Maker and its centrally governed framework resources. It retains no organisational authority after acceptance and no access through Edition Maker’s former keys, which are revoked at handover (F §§8–10).

Giving improvements back is voluntary; working data never enters the central Library (F §11.4).

**Branding:** using even one 49GAION idea, Library part, blueprint or Standard makes all the organisation’s products “Powered by 49GAION.” Later modification does not remove that rule. Only work containing nothing from 49GAION qualifies for self-only attribution; the definition says that is practically unavailable for work built on the framework (F §11).

The supplied READMEs identify text licensing as CC BY 4.0 and code licensing as Apache-2.0. They do not reproduce the referenced licence files or identify the copyright holder by name (H, “Rules of this repository”).

### 9. Does it fit one person and a group of companies?

Yes, by preserving the architecture while varying its size.

Every request becomes an AION through Edition Maker: “the size changes, the shape does not” (F §10.1).

For one person and one task:

- Distinct governance, management and accountability positions can share one holder where lawful.
- Several processes can share an AIOC.
- Independence requirements still apply; an outside independent position may be necessary.

The framework does not call this a separate “one-person organisation” type (F §§6, 15).

For a group:

- The group is one organisation with one group governance seat.
- A company retains its own governance seat where its law requires one.
- Each act is signed by a position of the company acting.
- AIOCs can contain other AIOCs to any depth while appearing externally as one unit.

**Sources:** F §§12.6, 16.

Scaling therefore changes composition and delegated responsibilities, not the basic distinction between human authority, AI design and operational execution.

### 10. Is it executable? Three strongest reasons and three biggest risks

**Assessment:** a specified implementation approach, not demonstrated operational readiness.

**Three strongest reasons**

1. **Buildable structure:** versioned blueprints, panels, API engines, cards, typed ports and owned Work Items define concrete components and interfaces (F §§8, 16–17).
2. **External control boundary:** gateway, signatures, authority ceilings and credential-revoking stops specify enforceable controls rather than merely asking AI to behave (F §20).
3. **Evidence-driven iteration:** fixed tests, independent challenge and recorded human promotion provide a correction mechanism (F §20; H, “Self-Improvement”).

**Three biggest risks**

1. **Incorrect AI output passes review.** **Answered as a mitigation, not eliminated:** independent engines, fixed tests, real-output evidence and human judgment; provisional tests exist before initial operation (F §20.5–6).
2. **AI exceeds authority or compromises its controls.** **Answered at specification level:** external gateway/stop, requester authority ceiling, signed widening and separately made control-code changes (F §20.1–4, 8–9).

The organisation starts small with several processes in one AIOC, and Top Management's one AIOC may stay the whole operation (F §15.1, §15.2.6).

A group of companies: one organisation with one governance seat over the group; where a company's law requires a governing body of its own, that body holds a governance seat for that company alone, and every act is signed by a position of the company that acts (F §12.6). An AIOC holds other AIOCs to any depth and is one piece from outside (F §16). The Library parts say the same: Target Operating Model serves "one person's work, a unit, a company, a group of companies, or a group with independent institutions inside it", designing only the hand-offs between companies (TOM README); Product Core is one product division "shared by every company it runs" (PC README).

### 10. Is it executable? Three reasons, three risks, and whether the text answers each

The text's own stance: 49GAION sets principles and few safeguards; "How each step is done is decided by AI at the time" (F §4; Part E).

Three strongest reasons it can work:
1. The safeguards are code outside every AI, not instructions to AI: the gateway holds credentials, logs every call, runs listed decisions only on a human signature and fails closed; the stop revokes credentials; the authority ceiling refuses acts beyond the original requester (F §13.1, §20.1–4, 9).
2. Structure over exhortation: cards, owned Work Items with acceptance tests, typed ports, checks on arrival, fixed tests in place before the first day, no step on one engine, done means visible evidence (M §3.13; F §8, §20.5–6; TOM README's review rule; TEST-DESIGN-CHAIN case 8).
3. A Library of ready-made parts, each with a card, a fixed test written before use and a mother blueprint pinned by commit and SHA-256, under CI checks; and a path that starts small and grows only on evidence (L; F §9, §15.1).

Three biggest risks:
1. Nothing has run. Every part is `DRAFT`, `NOT_RUN`; Edition Maker, the mandatory entry point, is "not yet written", "built later"; Engine Room is "named only" (L; every mother README: "Nothing here has run"). The text states this honestly but gives no plan, date or interim route. NOT IN THE TEXT: how an organisation gets an edition while Edition Maker does not exist. Not left to the `[ORG]AION` on purpose, since Edition Maker belongs to 49GAION (F §10.1). A gap.
2. AI wrong with confidence, and a weak human seat: the core proposes "whatever the knowledge of its holder" (F §13.2); the seat could sign without understanding. Answered as mitigation: two engines challenge, fixed tests on real outputs, success checked by someone other than the core, Chief of Staff never drops, raw record open (F §20.5–6, §13.3, §16); the residual is left to the organisation after handover (F §4 reading).
3. The organisation may loosen its own safeguards after handover, even the principles (F §12.3, §7, §20 reading). Answered on purpose: "hand over, then let go", within the law; "tighten freely, loosen only signed" (F §7, §20.4). Deliberate policy.

### 11. What would a new organisation ask that the text cannot answer?

- When can I get my edition, and what does it cost? NOT IN THE TEXT: Edition Maker is "built later" (L); the intake takes "money and time" (F §10.4) but no price, terms or delivery time is given.
- What exactly are the Standard's rules behind the pointers, the "one definition" of `[ORG]AION` in Standard §1.3 item 1, the Protocol sections §13 to §23, the Naming Standard, Edition Maker's PRD? Referenced throughout but not reproduced in the entry texts read. NOT IN THE TEXT as given.
- Which AI engines, hosting, security and data location will my organisation use, and what does the gateway cost to run? Left on purpose: "exact values and tools live in the Protocol of each `[ORG]AION`" (Protocol README); Engine Room not yet written (L).
- What is 49GAION's liability or warranty if the edition is defective after handover? The text says its authority ends and it keeps no access (F §10.7) but says nothing on liability. NOT IN THE TEXT.
- Who is the copyright holder? Pointed to LICENSE.md, not stated (H). NOT IN THE TEXT.
- How does the governance seat's "fresh live sign-in" work in practice, and who may hold a seat? Left to the organisation's own mechanism (M §3.8; F §4).
- What happens to my organisation if 49GAION's governing seat disappears? Answered in part: what was taken is held in its own Library and keeps working (F §12.4); whether the central repositories stay open is not promised beyond "stay open to it".
