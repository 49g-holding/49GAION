## What 49GAION takes from MASS, and what it does not

**Taken, as institutional rules:**

1. Every improvable process is written in four parts: role, instruction, contract, order.
2. The version in force is kept as champion until a challenger beats it; challengers change contracts and order first.
3. Challengers are judged pairwise against the champion, with reasons, and revised on the whole history of judgments.
4. Progress is measured only on held-out cases, by a judge whose verdicts never enter the loop.
5. The proposer gets the strongest model; progress is measured as quality per unit of cost.
6. What the best runs learnt is distilled into the defaults, so the process needs fewer special instructions over time.

**Not taken:**

1. **Fine-tuning model weights on the organisation's own runs.** The Standard distils into written defaults (roles, instructions, contracts, examples, fixed tests). Training a model is a separate decision of each organisation, outside this Standard, and stays subject to its Human Floor.
2. **One model in every role.** The research names this as its main risk. The Standard requires a judge separate from the proposer and an independent assurance unit.
3. **Self-judgment as the only signal.** Promotion always needs a human seat's approval (§2.2), the golden set and every fixed and Human Floor test.
4. **The reported gains as targets.** The results come from one preprint, one model family and research benchmarks; on two agentic coding benchmarks the gain per token was slightly below one. The Standard adopts the method and its safeguards, not the numbers.
