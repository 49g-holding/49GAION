## Decision

1. **Overseen: an irreversible action needs a clearance.** Under the Overseen link, an irreversible action (a sent message, a payment, a deletion) runs only on the overseeing position's clearance. A lapsed hold window never releases it: it stays held, and the lapse is raised to the overseeing position and to the position above it. A reversible action keeps the lapse rule of DR-0012. (Standard §5.2, the Overseen link and its rules; Protocol §4.10, item 18.)
2. **The Chief of Staff signs only with its position's grant.** Each human position sets up its own Chief of Staff and may grant it authority up to the right to sign for it; within that grant it signs for the position, and each such signature is tagged as a delegated act. A standing grant follows the rule of a standing approval: signed once by the position with a fresh live sign-in, it covers only acts that can be undone and do not widen access, budget or authority (the complete definition, §7); any other act needs the grant per action. The types of the Human Floor signature list stay with the position and need the position's own human signature (Standard §4.4, item 8). Where a text said that a Chief of Staff, or an attached Cell, never signs for its position, it now says that it signs only within that position's grant; the delegation ladder never gives that right, only the grant does. The complete definition says it in its Chief of Staff (§16), as the governing seat asked. (The complete definition, §16; Standard §1.7, §5.1, §5.2 and the glossary; Protocol §4.10; AIOC definition, Cells and Pods §1, item 9, and Standard Capabilities.)

A label is held by one position (§1.7, item 11). Customer ownership names item 7's Library and central repositories (§1.3, item 8). The naming example for a Cell is `[ORG]CELL-COSSL` (§1.6). The gateway and the stop are carried by the Charter and installed with the core, but run as code beside it, outside the reach of every AI (§4.4, item 11, and the glossary; the complete definition, §13, item 1).
